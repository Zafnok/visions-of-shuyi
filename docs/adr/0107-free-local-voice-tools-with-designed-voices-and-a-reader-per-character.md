# ADR-0107: Free local voice tools, with designed voices and a reader per character

- **Status:** Accepted
- **Date:** 2026-11-01
- **Related tickets:** 0043, 0721, 0722, 0754, 0755, 0756, 0757, 0476
- **Amends:** ADR-0046 rules 1, 3 and 5, for the cases below. Its other rules
  stand.

## Context

Nick chose the voices by ear in 0043
(`docs/design/voices-languages-and-script.md`, *Voices*): Qwen3-TTS for most
characters and Chatterbox for three, every voice picked per character. The
tools run on his machine (RTX 5090 Laptop, 24 GB). He also chose that a line
which says the player's name shows the name while the voice says another word.

Facts found while making the samples:

- Chatterbox can't design a voice. It reads from a reference clip, so its
  voices come from Qwen3-TTS's voice design model.
- A voice stays steady by reusing a fixed reference clip. Designing again with
  the same words gives a different voice.
- Neither reader takes a direction in words. Qwen3-TTS's clone mode reads the
  emotion from the words, and Chatterbox has one intensity setting.
- A clip pitched up or down sounds bad (Nick).
- On Windows: PyTorch needs the CUDA 12.8 builds for this card; flash-attn is
  not needed (`attn_implementation="sdpa"`); Hugging Face downloads must not
  use symbolic links without Developer Mode (`HF_HUB_DISABLE_SYMLINKS=1`);
  Qwen3-TTS's missing-SoX message is harmless. C: is nearly full, so
  everything stays on D:.

## Decision

1. **The voices are files in this repository.** One reference clip per cast
   entry, `assets-src/audio/voice-refs/<id>.flac`, with the words it says and
   how it was made in that folder's README. They are about 200 KB each,
   generated by us from a description, and they are the only thing that can't
   be remade, so they aren't left in a private folder or on one machine. This
   amends ADR-0046 rule 5 for reference clips only: generated lines stay in the
   private repository.
2. **A reader per cast entry.** `cast.ron` names each character's clip and
   reader: `Qwen3` (`Qwen/Qwen3-TTS-12Hz-1.7B-Base`, `generate_voice_clone`
   with `x_vector_only_mode=False`) or `Chatterbox`
   (`ChatterboxMultilingualTTS`, `t3_model="v3"`, `exaggeration` from 0.4 to
   0.9 by the line, `cfg_weight=0.4`). A new voice is designed with
   `Qwen/Qwen3-TTS-12Hz-1.7B-VoiceDesign`.
3. **One Python environment per tool**, Python 3.11 with `uv`, in
   `D:\voice-lab\envs\`, its models in `D:\voice-lab\hf\`. `cargo xtask voice`
   starts them as child processes (0721). They are developer tools, never in
   the game, CI or a dependency of a crate.
4. **Spoken words may differ from the shown words, only for the player's
   name.** A `.dlg` line says what the voice says in place of `{lead}` (0754).
   The manifest's `spoken` is that spoken text, and a clip is stale when it no
   longer matches the line's spoken text. This amends ADR-0046 rules 1 and 3,
   which said a clip plays only the words on screen. Keys and buttons don't get
   this: a line that names a key is silent text (0475).
5. **No pitch shifting.** A candidate voice is a new design. Loudness matching
   (ADR-0046, 0721) still applies.
6. **Other languages use the same clips and readers.** A reference clip reads
   other languages; a language may get its own clips if a cast round asks for
   them (0755, 0756).

## Consequences

- Free, offline and repeatable from the repository's clips, and no service can
  close under us. Three of the paid services surveyed have closed or are
  closing.
- Every line takes about 1.2 to 2.8 seconds of generating per second of speech,
  so Chapter 1 is under an hour plus retakes.
- Line directions (0752) have little effect with these readers. A human cast
  and a future tool can still use them.
- Chatterbox puts an inaudible watermark in everything it generates. Its terms
  don't require it. We don't remove it.
- Designed voices may still resemble someone by chance, and the tools don't
  publish their training data in detail. Rule 8 of ADR-0046 is met by never
  asking for a real person or character, and never using a tool's built-in
  voices.
- The two listening pages in `assets-src/audio/` reference clips that aren't in
  the repository; the published pages hold them.

## Alternatives considered

- **ElevenLabs or Gemini.** Passed the terms check, and Nick allowed paying if
  the free tools fell short. They didn't, so they stay the fallback.
- **VoxCPM2 and Ming-3.0-tts.** Heard in round 1, and not chosen.
- **One reader for everyone.** Simpler, but Nick heard Hollis best on
  Chatterbox and the lead and Harl best on Qwen3-TTS.
- **Keeping the voice's reference clips only in the private repository.** They
  aren't anyone else's work and hold nothing to protect, so a public copy costs
  nothing and can't go stale behind a pin.
- **Silent lines for the player's name.** Nick chose the stand-in.

Fits `docs/design/world-structure.md` and the outline's table of what

## What the chapter must do (the outline, `../outline.md`)

- **Goal:** reach Veyne Hall and confront Dace.
- **Conflict:** the roads of southern Ardeval are garrisoned by Hounds,
  and the lead is a named outlaw.
- **Turn:** Dace doesn't deny it: "I did what fed us. You had a house; I
  had a sister." Then Dace says, to Hollis: "You knew. You always knew."
  The lead learns Hollis kept the secret for four years. Dace escapes.
- **Arcs advanced:** the lead and Dace (face to face), Hollis (his
  secret is out), Tamsin (the paymaster's mark: her side quest's hook);
  from the sheets, Joss (his captain says his name and denies nothing),
  Rue (the boy from the cart, to his face), Gil (beaten, joins by his
  code; the first rule the party hears).
- **Leave for later:** what Hollis found and why he burned it (Chapter
  5: here he says only that he knew, and that he will tell); Wren alive
  (Chapter 6); the Door (Chapter 7); Dace's turn (Chapter 8 begins it).
  Chapter 4 ends with the lead in their own hall for one night, Dace
  alive on the road, and Hollis's confession owed.

## Dace retreats, and Hollis says one word

The outline fixes both: Dace escapes at the killing blow (0069), and
the lead learns at Veyne Hall that Hollis knew. This sheet puts the
confrontation **at the gate, before the battle**, where every player
hears it, not in a boss exchange only some players fight; the battle
exchanges with Dace (the lead, Hollis, Rue, Joss) are each about
something else. Hollis answers Dace with one word, "Aye", and the lead
with a promise to tell: his sheet gives the whole confession to Chapter
5's main story, the night before Saltmere, and this chapter must not
spend it. The crack between them is this chapter's; the mending is
Chapter 5's.

## Scenes

### 1. The valley road (`ch04_intro`)

- **Where:** the morning the party leaves the lake behind: the forest
  road comes out above the heartland, and the valley opens under them,
  stubble, orchards, stone farms, a crossroads with a black pennant on
  a post. Plays when the chapter opens (the world map appears). Music:
  `talk_calm`.
- **Who:** the lead, Aske, Joss, Tamsin, Hollis, Maud, Rue (each in an
  `@if`).
- **What happens:** Aske on the fields: a hold could eat for a year off
  one of them, and she is not praising anything. Joss walks backward
  telling them where his ma's farm is (two valleys over, by the ford
  with the willow) and that he knows he can't, the twins would tell the
  whole lane by noon, and he's sorry he keeps saying it. Hollis: he's
  not sorry, and eat something, you've talked since dawn. Tamsin reads
  the paper off the pennant post as they pass: the price on the lead,
  which she won't say aloud ("it's not enough"), and it goes in the book
  under the lead's name. Maud has the register wrapped in her spare robe
  and asks if it will rain. Rue, bread in hand, says the first thing to
  Aske since the scriptorium, about the pot (done slow, the way Aske told her at the lake), and gets nothing back
  (Aske still isn't speaking to her). Hollis: posts at every
  crossroads and not one has moved; every one of us walking past in
  daylight and nobody has stood up; the boy's not stopping us, he's
  letting us come. They go down.
- **Purpose:** the valley as Hound country; the goal and the terms (the
  roads are his, and open); Joss not okay; Aske and Rue still broken;
  Hollis reading his son.
- **Changes:** *information* (the price exists; the roads are posted and
  open on purpose), *stakes* (they are walking into his hands, and know
  it).

### 2. Aldwater Toll Bridge (`ch04_aldwater_intro`, `ch04_aldwater_victory`)

- **Where:** the crown's bridge over the Aldwater, a toll-bar across it,
  Hounds at both ends, a line of carts waiting with their beasts
  steaming. Guards the path to the hall. Music: the `skirmish` pool
  (the battle file's).
- **Who:** Tamsin, Joss, Aske, Hollis, Maud, Rue, Gil if he has joined
  (the player may do Millhaven Road first).
- **Before (3–6):** the sergeant at the bar shouts it across the water
  (narration): the exile passes untouched, the captain's orders; the
  rest hand over their steel and the two books from the abbey and walk
  behind. Tamsin: a Hound bar on the king's bridge, and that chest never
  sees Varenhall. Joss knows the man at the bar (he taught Joss the bar
  drill his first week) and is going to be recognized. Hollis: nobody
  hands over steel; the books stay in the middle. The bar comes down.
- **After (2–5):** Tamsin in the toll-chest: coin, a tally of every
  cart, and a mark she knows from three years of pay-notes that came
  back "no funds": the crown paymaster's, and the office it names is in
  Millhaven; 4,212 crowns he never found, and his mark on a Hound
  toll-bar. Aske: give the carters their toll back. Tamsin: "...Half."
  Half, and the tally is hers. **The hook for 1072** (*The Company Ledger*): the
  paymaster who pocketed the Red Company's wages has an office in
  Millhaven and his mark on a Hound toll; the quest's node unlocks when
  this bridge is won, with Tamsin alive. 1072 names him and decides the
  rest.
- **Changes:** *information* (a toll in nobody's name; the paymaster's
  mark), *plan* (the path to the hall is open).

### 3. Millhaven Road (`ch04_millhaven_road_intro`, Gil's battle lines, `ch04_millhaven_road_victory`)

- **Where:** a sunny road into the market town: a tipped pie cart, pies
  in the dust, a Hound patrol in a ring around a bare-chested man with a
  red sash who is addressing them like a crowd, the pie-seller behind
  him. Optional. Music: the `skirmish` pool.
- **Before (3–6):** Gil announces himself to the road, the party
  included: his name and title, the wager (his last purse that he beats
  every man on this road before the Millhaven bell), and rule four (you
  do not walk past a kicked-over cart). The party's arrival makes them
  men on the road; the bet covers them; he is sorry about it and
  completely serious. Tamsin, on the mare: she isn't a man; does she
  count? ("You count, ma'am. I'm sorry for it.") Hollis looks at how he
  stands: let him come to the spears.
- **In the fight:** `ch04_prizefighter_engage` (anyone): a formal
  challenge, one box, one rule. `ch04_prizefighter_fell` (beaten, so he
  joins): "Well struck", and rule two: a beaten man serves the one who
  beat him until a fair rematch; he leaves the map and waits at the end.
  No talk conversations (no recruiting by talking).
- **After, if he was beaten (6–12, a quiet scene):** he bows to the
  lead, "Champion": the lead's people beat him, which is the lead
  beating him, and he is theirs until a fair rematch on a fitting day
  before a crowd; then he'll ask, not before. The party reacts as
  people: Tamsin saw him bow to the mare first; Maud sits him on the
  cart for his bleeding ear; Rue has counted three pies into him
  already; he bows to Aske and she doesn't know what to do with it; Joss
  counts the ribbons on his sash, six, and sees the bare place, which
  Gil touches. **Choice (Gil's):**
  the lead answers his oath (accept it plainly / wry / an act: hand him
  a pie); each reply moves him. **Planted:** the bare seventh place, and
  his plan for after, said once: a fair of his own, a ring and a pie
  stand and one rule over the gate, no betting. Rue: he eats more than
  she does, and she has never met anyone who could.
- **After, if he wasn't (1–2):** narration: the big man settled his bet
  with the pie-seller and walked toward the town bell, counting
  something on his belt; nobody stopped him. He is missed for good.
- **Changes:** *relationship* (a new companion, by a rule), and nothing
  in the plot.

### 4. The rise above the hall (`ch04_eve`)

- **Where:** a hill above the valley at dusk into dark, the night before:
  below, the lights of Veyne Hall, the kitchen window late, the gate
  lantern, the long wall, the mill road black. No fire (Hound country);
  breath showing. Plays before the Veyne Hall battle, before the gate.
  Music: `scene_sad`.
- **Who:** the lead, Hollis, Maud, Aske, Tamsin, Joss, Rue, Gil (each in
  an `@if`).
- **What happens:** Hollis names the lit windows for the lead like a man
  reading a letter: the kitchen at the end, the cook always kept it
  late; the study dark, the boy's not in it. Aske: the lead grew
  up in that? Her whole hold would fit in the yard, goats and all;
  "...Noble." Maud takes the lead's blue hands and puts them in her
  sleeves. Hollis: not the gate. The laundry door behind the long wall,
  down the river meadow; it never locked, it only looked locked, and
  everybody in the house believed it but two children; these two, at
  nine, out to the mill pond and back with their boots full, and he
  caught them; that's the way in. Joss starts on what the captain will
  and won't do (he walks the wall at night, he checks the men's boots)
  and Tamsin stops him, gently, with the mare's head to hold while she
  does the girth. Gil (if there) has put himself on the windward side of the lead
  and says nothing about it. Then Hollis: "Kid. Tomorrow, whatever he
  says to you--" and stops. **Choice 1:** the lead's reply ("Say it
  now." / "Tomorrow, then." / silence); each way he can't, and each way
  his hands do something (the strap, the spear, the lead's shoulder),
  and he gives an order about sleep. Rue, last, to the lead: she saw
  that; did the lead?
- **Purpose:** warmth before the threat in the lead's own country (the
  windows, the hands, the two children at the mill pond); the plan said
  once by the man who knows the house; Hollis's secret a day away;
  Joss's captain in the lit house. Nobody is wrong here: the scene's
  engines are worry and care.
- **Changes:** *plan* (the laundry door), *relationship* (Hollis nearly
  tells; the lead and Rue have the same question now).

### 5. The gate (`ch04_gate`)

- **Where:** the gate of Veyne Hall at gray dawn, opened from inside
  before anybody knocks. Dace in the gateway, bare-headed, Hounds behind
  him in the yard. Music: `talk_antagonist`.
- **Who:** the lead, Dace; Hollis, Rue, Joss, Maud, Tamsin, Aske, Gil
  (each in an `@if`; Dace needs none).
- **What happens:** Dace, warm and quick: the lead's name, twice; the
  coat with the hole in it; nobody has slept since the lake; the
  kitchen has been lit since four, come in, all of you; "What's that in
  your hand? No. I know what that is." He has not looked at Hollis.
  **Choice 2:** how the lead puts the pay-book to him (earnest: tell me
  it isn't you in that book / blunt: eleven children, your name / wry:
  the columns need work); his first answer differs by the tone, then
  the scene rejoins on the thing itself. **He
  doesn't deny it:** Vosse had his name in his hand and a cart with an
  empty place in it; he did what fed us; you had a house, I had a
  sister; they ate better in the Vigil's school, some of them. Then he
  looks past the lead, at last: "And you. Behind them. You knew. You
  always knew." He never asked where the boots came from. Hollis:
  "...Aye." Joss: "Captain?" and nothing after it. Dace, colder: the
  register and the pay-book, and walk south today, and he'll write to
  Ossary that they burned with the prior; take the old man with you.
  The lead: "No." Dace, cold: "Then take it, Veyne. I'll be in the
  hall." The gate shuts. (Rue's beat with him, the belt and the crying,
  is the battle's: `ch04_boss_engage_heretic`.)
- **Purpose:** the outline's turn, heard by every player: Dace face to
  face, no denial, "you knew"; the lead learns Hollis kept it four
  years; why this fight (he wants the books and the lead gone; the lead
  wants the house, or him).
- **Changes:** *information* (Dace confirms the pay-book; Hollis knew),
  *relationship* (the lead and Hollis crack; the lead and Dace are
  enemies by his word), *plan* (take the hall).

### 7. The stable (`ch04_victory`)

- **Where:** the yard of the hall, late morning, quiet; the Hounds who
  didn't run are in the cellar; the kitchen fire is still lit. Then the
  stable, the end stall, the bay mare. Music: `scene_tragic`.
- **Who:** the lead, Maud, Joss, Aske, Tamsin, Gil, Rue, Hollis (each in
  an `@if`).
- **What happens:** Maud does hands on the mounting block. Joss sits on
  the step and doesn't say "the captain", for the first time. Aske: it
  is a good house, she did not say that. Tamsin: they have till his
  riders reach the county seat, a day, and the kitchen is full, so
  eat; Rue has already been through it (there were eggs). Rue, to the
  lead: he's in the stable; go on; she isn't doing it for the lead this
  time, heir. The stable: Hollis's back, a brush, the bay mare fat in
  the end stall. He says it without turning: aye, he knew; not all of
  it, but enough; he'll tell the lead all of it, what he found and what
  he did with it, not today, and not in his stable. **Choice 3:** the lead's reply ("Four
  years." / "Tell me now." / an act: take the brush); each way he gives
  way a little and does the mare. Then the one true thing sideways:
  nobody has ridden her since Hollis left; the boy wouldn't let them;
  he kept her fed and shod. The lead: "We leave tonight." Hollis: watch
  her off side, she kicks, she always did.
- **Purpose:** Hollis's secret out between them, and the confession
  owed (Chapter 5); the mare as the one thing Dace did for his father;
  the others as people after a fight in a house that isn't theirs.
- **Changes:** *relationship* (the lead and Hollis: cracked, and a
  promise), *plan* (leave tonight; where is Chapter 5's), *information*
  (Dace kept the mare).
- **Every path:** without Hollis, the stable scene can't play: the lead
  finds the bay mare alone (narration), and nobody says "aye". Without
  Rue, nobody sends the lead; the lead goes.

## Continuity notes

- Southern Ardeval, the valley heartland: a week's walking from the
  lake. The Aldwater is the river the crown's bridge crosses on the way
  to the hall (`place.aldwater.river`, "the Aldwater", a short form of
  the bridge's name). Millhaven is a market town off the hall road;
  Millhaven Road is the road into it. Joss's family farm is "two
  valleys over", in Hound country; nobody goes.
- Veyne Hall, as the scripts have shown it: the gate and its guardroom
  (Chapter 2), the yard with the mounting block (Chapter 3), the old
  lord's study with the locked drawer (Chapter 1; untouched here), and
  now the long wall, the river meadow, the laundry door into the
  servants' passage (never locked, only looked locked), the kitchen,
  the stable with the bay mare in the end stall, and the mill pond the
  two children got out to at nine. The mill itself (the night at the
  mill) is in the valley; nobody goes there in this chapter.
- Nobody says how many on the wall, how much the toll, what the price
  on the lead is: the map, the objective and Tamsin's refusal carry it.
- The pay-book is read to Dace as "D. Marr, informer: eleven children";
  he confirms the count and the year. He does not say "I'm sorry" (his
  sheet: not until Act 3).
- Hollis says "the boy" throughout, and "Aye" once to Dace. He does not
  say what he found or why he burned it (Chapter 5).
- Dace's retreat is by the laundry door; the Hounds who didn't follow
  him are in the cellar. He is wounded (the arm) and alive on the
  Millhaven road that night.
- Gil, if beaten, is with the party from the Millhaven Road victory
  scene on; if not, he is never seen again. He calls the lead
  "Champion". His cousin is never mentioned (his sheet).
- Tamsin's 4,212 crowns are her own number (her sheet), said once, at
  the toll-chest.
- The lead's name: `{lead}`; "Veyne" and every other name is a token.
  Pronoun tokens only. No gendered words.
- The plain build has no portrait for anyone but the lead, so the script
  uses only the five expressions every character has.

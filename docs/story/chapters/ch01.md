# Chapter 1: The Letter (beat sheet)

Source: ticket 0701. Script: ticket 0707 (`assets/dialogue/ch01.dlg`),
played straight by 0724 and reworked scene by scene by 0728, which added
the **Wants** line to each scene: what each speaker is after in it, which
is where a line's angle on a fact comes from.
Fits [`docs/design/chapter-1.md`](../../design/chapter-1.md): **one battle,
Rout, no turn limit**, lord's fall = game over, 6 fixed player units, 12–14
enemies + 1 named boss, **no extras** (no talk-recruit, village, chest,
reinforcements, scripted terrain magic, elementals or battle notes), no
Preparations, fixed pack.

in a pale grey tunic.

## Budget (from 0707)

- Opening + pre-battle: **≤ ~40 text boxes** in total.
- In-battle lines: 1–4 boxes each.
- Victory: **≤ ~20 boxes**.
- **Reply choices: 2–3** in the chapter (`setting-and-tone.md`: about 1–3 per
  chapter). The lead speaks only in choices and short neutral lines.

## What the chapter must do

- **Main plot:** someone powerful wants the lead, Hollis and Rue dead, and the
  kill order bears the Veyne seal (Dace). The lead breaks exile to find out
  why.
- **Personal arcs:** Hollis (his son's letter, then his son's seal), Tamsin
  (kills her old captain), Maud (realises her report brought the killers),
  Rue (recognises the seal, and suspects the lead).
- **Leave for later:** Dace's child-selling (Chapter 3), Hollis's secret
  (Chapter 4), Wren being alive (Chapter 6), the Door (Chapter 7). Chapter 1
  only plants questions.

## Scenes

### 0. Opening caption (part of `ch01_intro`)

- **What:** at most 3 narration boxes over a dark screen: "Three years after
  the Ash Peace. The war's soldiers were never paid. In the Thornmarch, far
  from the capital, an exile chops wood." No lore dump: the Ashfields, the
  Vigil and the Door are **not** explained here.
- **Purpose:** place and mood.
- **Changes:** nothing yet. That's why it's three boxes, not more.

### 1. The letter (`ch01_intro`)

- **Where:** Harrowby, the yard behind the lead's rented house, at dawn.
  Caption: `Harrowby, the Thornmarch`.
- **Who:** the lead, Hollis.
- **Beats:**
  1. Hollis and the lead spar with wooden swords, four years of it. Played
     straight (0724): he corrects the lead's guard, the lead disarms him for
     the first time, and he says so. Shows their family-like bond without
     explaining it. No banter: the game's opening is not where the jokes go.
     (0728: he has had the letter in his coat all night and drilled the lead
     anyway, for one more ordinary morning. He admits he has spent the
     morning not handing it over; the first disarm in four years lands on
     the worst morning in four years, and he says "that's good" before he
     says anything else.)
  2. Hollis hands over a letter a peddler brought last night. It's Dace's
     handwriting, his first letter in four years: *"Leave the march. Tonight. They
     are coming for everyone who was there that night. Burn this. -D."*
     (0707: the script's letter is unsigned. Hollis knows the hand, and the
     lead says the name he won't.)
  3. Hollis recognises his son's writing. His face (`sad` or `weary`) says
     more than his words.
  4. **Choice 1** (the lead's reaction; sample in the lead's sheet): earnest
     "He's warning us" / wry "Four years of silence, and now he's urgent" /
     blunt "What does he want?". Hollis reacts differently to each, and the
     scene rejoins.
- **Purpose:** establish the exile, Hollis, and Dace as absent but important.
- **Wants (0728):** Hollis wants one more ordinary morning, then wants the
  lead to understand the letter without his having to say his son's name;
  what he notices is the four years of silence, and that the letter says
  "everyone", which includes a dead girl. The lead wants to know what Dace
  means (the player picks how).
- **Changes:** *information* (danger is coming; Dace knows), *relationship*
  (a crack in Hollis's calm the player can see).

### 2. The Red Company (`ch01_prebattle`)

- **Where:** Harrowby's green by the chapel, minutes later.
- **Who:** the lead, Hollis, Tamsin, Maud, Rue, Aske. They come in one by
  one, a line or two each: a character intro, not a speech.
- **Beats:**
  1. **Tamsin** rides in hard: Red Harl's company took the old toll-fort at
     the ford overnight. He tried to hire her back. He was showing a paper
     with a lord's seal and asking for "the exile, the old man, and the girl
     with the burned hands". She reports it like a sergeant; no ledger joke
     (0724). What she noticed (0728): his thumb stayed over the seal the
     whole time, the paper describes three people instead of naming them,
     and the price by each would feed his company till spring.
  2. **Maud** hurries up with **Rue**. At "burned hands", Maud
     goes pale (`flustered`): she wrote to the Vigil about Rue this autumn. She
     *doesn't* say so aloud; the player sees it only in her expression and
     one half-finished line. Rue notices everyone looking at her hands and
     glares.
  3. **Aske** drops out of a tree or comes out of the woods, having counted
     them: fourteen men in the fort, three with bows (the map has three
     Archers, `chapter-1.md`). She counted at dusk and again at first light,
     and she judges them as a hunter does (0728): they shot many arrows at
     the deer from the wall and most hit nothing; they killed three deer,
     carried off one and left two for the crows. She wants them gone
     because they're poaching *her* deer, and because they waste. That's
     her cover for liking the hamlet. Maud asks Rue to hide in the chapel
     cellar; Rue refuses: she is sworn to fire, and she'll fight.
  4. Hollis goes inside and comes back with the two real swords that have
     been under the floor for four years. Breaking exile is now physical: the
     lead picks up steel.
  5. **Choice 2** (the plan): earnest "We keep them away from the village" /
     wry "Only fourteen? I'm insulted." / blunt "We hit the fort first."
     Tamsin, Aske and Rue each react to one option. Every path fights.
     Tamsin closes it: anyone who gets away comes back tonight with torches,
     so it has to be all of them.
- **Purpose:** why this fight (they're coming for us, and the village is in
  the way), plus the cast's first impressions and targets.
- **Wants (0728):** Tamsin wants the Captain and the lead to understand
  what Harl is and that he has to be finished; she rode up to the gate
  because the company was hers once, and rode back because of what he
  offered. Maud wants to help, then wants Rue in the cellar, then wants
  nobody to ask her what she meant. Rue wants the eyes off her hands and
  wants to fight rather than hide again. Aske wants the Red Company out of
  her woods and says exactly what she counted and what she thinks of men
  who waste arrows and meat. Hollis wants nothing said until there is steel
  in the lead's hand, and then wants the lead to decide.
- **Changes:** *plan* (fight), *stakes* (Rue is a named target; Maud's guilt
  seeded), *relationship* (a party exists).
- **Exposition check:** each character's backstory is a single hint, and the
  rest is left for later chapters.

### 3. The battle (Rout)

- **Map:** the `chapter-1.md` sketch. The party starts in Harrowby at the
  bottom left. The river crosses the map with a bridge on the road. A forest
  lane is the slower route. Harl holds the old toll-fort at the top right.
  0803 designs the real map.
- **Why Rout makes sense:** anyone who survives comes back tonight with
  torches. Harl's company has to be broken, not chased off.
- **In-battle moments (scene ids from 0707):**
  - `ch01_boss_engage`, Harl's lines when a unit engages him:
    - default: a sour soldier's line;
    - **vs the lead**: "Nothing personal, Your Grace. You're three hundred
      crowns...";
    - **vs Tamsin**: "Rook. You left before pay-day. Bad habit.", and her
      reply.

    (0705 supports them: `ch01_boss_engage`, `ch01_boss_engage_lead` and
    `ch01_boss_engage_sergeant`. 0707 also wrote `ch01_boss_half`, the
    half-HP line every boss has, `battle-scenes-and-recruitment.md`.)
  - **Harl's death quote:** "The new lord of Veyne pays in good silver...
    Your own crest on the order, and everything. Must be nice, having
    friends." It's said *to the lead*, and it hands over the chapter's
    question. (Harl never met Dace. He knows the seal and the silver, nothing
    more.)
  - **Death quotes** for each of the five companions (Classic: they're gone
    for good), and the lead's fall line (game over). Each is in character
    (see the sheets) and at most 2 boxes. 0707 added a **retreat line** for
    each companion for Casual (`ch01_retreat_<id>`,
    `death-and-difficulty.md`): hurt, not dying. Each says what that
    person notices as they fall (0728): Hollis his leg and the lead's
    guard, Tamsin the saddle, Aske the arm that draws the bow, Maud that
    she can't mend anyone, Rue the fire shaking in her hands.
  - Optionally, one short line at the start of the first Player Phase: Hollis,
    "Stay close, kid. The first real one's always ugly. Guard up, and let
    them come to us." This isn't a tutorial: help and tips are ticket
    0406's job.
- **Wants (0728):** Harl wants to be paid and, failing that, to hurt: he
  talks in prices to everyone, and his last words give the lead the one
  thing he knows (the seal, the silver) because it will hurt. The
  companions want the lead to finish it without them.
- **No talk-recruit, villages or chests** (`chapter-1.md`).

### 4. After the fight (`ch01_victory`)

- **Where:** the toll-fort, at noon. Smoke from one burned barn.
- **Who:** all six.
- **Beats:**
  1. Tamsin stands over Harl and marks him in her ledger, paid in full,
     without a smile. She closes his eyes. One thing slips out before she
     shuts the door on it (0728): "He taught me to ride." Then: it was a
     job. (Her arc: the job she said it was.)
  2. Harl's orders, found on his body: payment for "the exile, the old man,
     the girl", sealed in wax with **the Veyne crest**. The lead's own family
     seal, which only the holder of the title (Dace) can use.
  3. Hollis looks at the seal and says nothing at all. Tamsin fills the
     silence.
  4. **Rue**, cold: "I've seen that seal before, heir. On a cart. At night.
     With children in it." She doesn't explain. Four years ago the seal meant
     Lord Veyne's house, so as far as she knows, the lead's family ran the
     cart. Suspicion is now on the lead. (The truth, that Dace stamped the
     passes, comes in Chapter 3.)
  5. Maud: they can't stay. The Vigil knows Rue is here, and they'll come
     again. (She still doesn't confess the report; that's Chapter 3.)
  6. Aske: she's coming, "because you owe me eleven arrows" (the scene's one
     light moment, 0724). Tamsin: "as far as there's pay", then, quieter,
     "further, probably".
  7. **Choice 3** (optional; keep it if the chapter's total stays ≤ 3):
     leaving the Thornmarch, which breaks exile. Earnest "We find Dace and ask
     him" / wry "I've always wanted to be an outlaw" / blunt "Veyne Hall. We
     leave now."
- **Purpose:** the consequences of the fight, and the chapter's question
  (why would Dace do this?), turned into the Act 1 goal.
- **Wants (0728):** Tamsin wants Harl to have been a job, and fills every
  silence. Hollis wants to say nothing about the seal and then wants the
  lead to go to his son, outlaw or not, without his saying the name. Rue
  wants to say what she saw and watch the lead's face while she says it.
  Maud wants them gone before the Vigil sends others, without confessing
  why she knows it will. Aske wants a reason to come that isn't liking
  them (the arrows).
- **Changes:** *information* (the Veyne seal; Rue has seen it before),
  *relationship* (Rue distrusts the lead; Hollis's silence), *plan* (leave
  for Veyne Hall; the lead is now an outlaw).
- **If a companion died in the battle** (Classic; 0716): the scene plays
  without them, and their absence is felt rather than explained. Three
  things reach the player on every path: the order bears the Veyne seal,
  only Dace may use it, and they leave for Veyne Hall. Rue's suspicion is
  hers alone: without her it isn't said. Hollis, if he lived, names each
  of the dead before he goes for the shovel; if he didn't, the lead buries
  them. What each death leaves unsaid is in the ledger ("If a companion
  died in Chapter 1").

### 5. To be continued (`ch01_tbc`)

- **Where:** Veyne Hall, that night. Caption: `Veyne Hall`.
- **Who:** Dace, and a Vigil messenger (0707 chose the messenger over Crane
  himself: it keeps Crane's first appearance for later).
- **Beats:** Dace reads a report in the lead's father's old study, under the
  Veyne banner. The messenger: "The Master of Vows asks whether it's done."
  Dace, after a beat: "Tell him it's done." The messenger leaves. Dace, alone,
  looks at the blue sash with the full Veyne crest, the crest the lead cut
  off their coat. At most 6 boxes. (0707: the report says the Red Company is
  dead and the exile is not; Dace burns it, and his last line is to the lead:
  "I told you to run.")
- **Purpose:** show the rival's face and his doubleness (he lies to his own
  masters) and name the Master of Vows without explaining him. The player now
  knows more than the lead does.
- **Wants (0728):** the messenger wants a yes or a no. Dace wants the
  report to say something else, and when it doesn't, wants the lead to
  have done as he said.
- **Changes:** *information for the player*: Dace sent the killers, is
  covering for the lead's survival, and answers to someone in the Vigil.

## Continuity notes for 0707

- Harrowby: forty people, a chapel, a mill, a ford. The toll-fort is an old
  ruin, and the Red Company arrived the night before.
- The lead and Hollis have lived there four years. Maud has been keeper for
  six. Rue arrived this autumn. Tamsin has guarded the hamlet since last
  winter. Aske poaches the woods and sells game to the hamlet.
- Wren is **not mentioned by name** in Chapter 1, except perhaps once by
  Hollis, and only as "my girl". She's for later chapters.
- The lead's name: `{lead}` token; the family name "Veyne" is plain text. Use
  pronoun tokens only (0708). No gendered words ("lad", "lass", "my lord",
  "my lady"). Harl says "Your Grace" as sarcasm; that's gender-neutral.
- Everyone's names in dialogue text go through the names table (see
  [`../names.md`](../names.md) and ticket 0709), so renames stay one-line
  changes.

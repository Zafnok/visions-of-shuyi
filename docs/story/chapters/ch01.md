# Chapter 1: The Letter (beat sheet)

Source: ticket 0701 (the outline's Chapter 1 entry, `../outline.md`).
Script: `assets/dialogue/ch01.dlg`, written by 0707, reworked by 0724
and 0728, and **written again from the outline on a blank page by 0737**
(the `story-writing` skill, *Rewrites start on a blank page*). Nick,
2026-10-25, on 0737's first draft: it "still reads semantically the same
as before", because that draft had been written from this sheet, and by
then this sheet was the old script in outline form. So the scenes below
are now written **from the script**, after it, as a record of what each
scene does: the beats Nick agreed are the outline's (goal, conflict,
turn, arcs) and the sheets' arcs, and the staging here is the writer's,
to be thrown away by the next rewrite. A line this sheet quotes says
what a moment gets across, never the words to use.

Fits [`docs/design/chapter-1.md`](../../design/chapter-1.md): **one battle,
defeat Harl, no turn limit**, lord's fall = game over, 6 fixed player units,
13 soldiers + 1 named boss, **no extras** (no talk-recruit, village, chest,
reinforcements, scripted terrain magic, elementals or battle notes), no
Preparations, fixed pack.

## What the chapter must do (the outline, `../outline.md`)

- **Goal:** survive. The Red Company has come to Harrowby to kill the
  lead, Hollis and "the girl with burned hands".
- **Conflict:** Red Harl's Unpaid band holds the old toll-fort by the
  ford, and the lead has no army, only five people who happen to be
  there.
- **Turn:** Harl's orders carry the **Veyne seal**, which only Dace holds.
  Rue has seen it before, on the passes of Vosse's cart four years ago,
  so she suspects House Veyne, and the lead. Dace sent the warning *and*
  the killers.
- **Arcs advanced:** Hollis (his son's hand, then his son's seal, and
  says nothing), Tamsin (kills her old captain and calls it a job), Maud
  (her report brought them), Rue (the seal).
- **Leave for later:** Dace's child-selling (Chapter 3), Hollis's secret
  (Chapter 4), Wren being alive (Chapter 6), the Door (Chapter 7).
  Chapter 1 only plants questions: why is Rue on the paper; why does
  Dace hold the lead's title; who is the Master of Vows.

## Scenes (as the script stages them; 0737)

### 0. Opening captions (part of `ch01_intro`)

- **What:** three narration boxes over a dark screen: three years after
  the Ash Peace, the war's soldiers never paid, some still on the roads
  under their old captains; an exile up before dawn in a yard in the
  Thornmarch. No lore dump: the Ashfields, the Vigil and the Door are
  **not** explained here. The title cinematic shows these three boxes
  (`assets/cinematics/title.ron`), so they stay first.
- **Purpose:** place and mood.

### 1. An ordinary morning (`ch01_intro`)

- **Where:** the yard behind the rented house, at dawn (the
  `harrowby_yard` map, with the `ch01_sparring` opening).
- **Who:** the lead, Hollis.
- **What happens:** the lead splits wood; Hollis mends the lead's boot on
  the block by the fire, and has not slept: there is a folded paper in
  his coat that he keeps touching, and nothing is said about it. He
  corrects the lead's axe, asks for the other boot, and says he has
  never seen the sea in forty years on his feet, because it is the one
  thing the boy wrote home about from the war that he believed (the
  plant for Chapter 8, Saltmere). **Choice 1**, what the lead will do
  when the exile lifts: Hollis answers each warmly or goes quiet. The
  chapel bell starts an hour early. "That's not the hour. Get the
  swords. Under the floor."
- **Purpose:** warmth before the threat (the craft research: the deaths
  that land are set up by a scene with only small things at stake); the
  exile and Hollis; Dace as absent and important; the letter present and
  unread; the lead sent for steel.
- **Changes:** *relationship* (a plan for after, said once), *the lead
  bears steel*. The danger is not yet said.

### 2. The Red Company (`ch01_prebattle`)

- **Where:** by the chapel, minutes later (the battle map, where the
  party starts).
- **Who:** the lead, Maud, Tamsin, Rue, Hollis, Aske. People talking,
  not a line each to the lead.
- **What happens:** Maud found a paper nailed to the chapel door and
  rang the bell: the village burns at noon unless three people walk up
  to the toll-fort, "the exile, the old man, the girl with the burned
  hands". The description of Rue is a keeper's phrasing, and Maud hears
  her own report in it; she says only that a keeper wrote it and her
  hands won't hold it. (Her realisation; no confession.) Tamsin takes
  it: Harl's writing on somebody else's paper; he will do it, he burned
  a farm last winter for less. Rue, on the paper, offers to walk up
  herself with her hands out of her sleeves; Tamsin asks what she has to
  do with any of it; "Why don't you ask whoever wrote it?" Then Hollis
  takes the folded paper out of his coat: the letter that came by
  peddler in the night, unsigned (get out of the march by tonight; they
  are coming for everyone who was at the mill that night; burn this).
  Tamsin asks whose hand; Hollis won't say; the lead does, in front of
  everyone: Dace's. "Aye. Burn it. Somebody. I can't." Rue takes it and
  it is ash in her fingers, and she is looking at the lead. Aske: nobody
  goes up; they ate a deer raw last night and won't wait for noon.
  Hollis: the ford is the only road out and they are sitting on it; the
  hamlet into the chapel; we have steel. **Choice 2** (the plan): Maud,
  Tamsin or Aske answers. Tamsin: he stands on walls, she will go around
  by the river.
- **Purpose:** why this fight (they are coming for us, and the hamlet
  burns if we don't); the letter and the name, heard by all six; Maud's
  guilt seeded; Rue a named target with a reason nobody knows, and the
  first look she gives the lead; Rue's fire shown.
- **Changes:** *information* (danger today; Dace wrote the warning; the
  party heard the name), *plan* (fight, now), *stakes* (the hamlet at
  noon), *relationship* (a party exists; Hollis asks for help with the
  one thing he can't do). **No letter exists after this scene.**
- **Note:** the title cinematic shows this scene's first box (Maud's
  news), so it stays first.

### 3. The battle (defeat Harl)

- **Map:** `chapter-1.md` as built by 0803. The party starts at the
  hamlet; the toll-fort is at the top right; Harl holds its fort tile.
- **Objective: defeat Harl** (0845). Tamsin's line that the men are his
  and will run when he falls comes after the battle, by what they do.
- **In-battle scenes** (ids from 0707, wired by `battles/ch01.ron`):
  - `ch01_first_turn`: Hollis, one box: boots holding? keep the road
    under you, the fort's not going anywhere.
  - `ch01_boss_engage`: Harl to anyone not on his paper: forty men he
    brought home from a war and the crown gave them a road; go home
    before he takes it out on you.
  - `ch01_boss_engage_lead`: you could have walked up like the paper
    said; three hundred a head, somebody's paying for you.
  - `ch01_boss_engage_sergeant`, four boxes: who's that on the gray,
    Rook, he had her down as dead in a ditch; get off the wall; he put
    her on her first horse and can put her off it; she held the horses
    at that farm after, and watched.
  - `ch01_boss_half`: should have cooked the deer; still standing.
  - `ch01_death_red_captain`: first the plea (don't hang the boys, they
    were hungry), then to the lead: a lord bought you, your own wax on
    the paper, silver up front, ask the new one in your hall what you
    cost him.
  - Death quotes, about the living, each a different shape: Hollis (stay
    in your line; the sea, tell the boy that and not the rest), Tamsin
    (the mare, don't let her run back to him), Aske (she counted the wall
    wrong; the silver in her left boot, north), Maud (ring the bell so
    they come out of the chapel), Rue (who will find the others from the
    barracks; why is it cold). Retreat lines (Casual): the other knee, "fill the
    gap"; off the horse and nobody saw; the quiver handed over; the
    chapel; the fire that won't light.
  - The lead's fall: one narration box; game over.

### 4. After the fight (`ch01_victory`)

- **Where:** the toll-fort, noon. The gate open; the rest of the company
  ran through it when Harl fell.
- **Who:** all six (each in an `@if` block; 0716).
- **What happens:** Rue, going through the packs for food, finds the
  order in Harl's coat (three lines, a price, the seal); the lead names
  it: the Veyne seal, the crest the lead cut off their coat. The last time she saw that wax it was on a cart of children at
  night: "Your house's wax. Did you know?" **Choice 3**: the lead answers
  the charge (earnest, wry or blunt) and Rue answers back, unconvinced.
  Hollis looks at the wax and goes and sits on the step with his back to
  everyone. Tamsin, to the lead: give him a minute; it's the boy's; the
  hall, the title and the wax went to him with the king's thanks. Then
  she goes to the body so as not to look at Hollis: cuts the red cloth
  off Harl's head, keeps it, writes one word in her ledger, and asks
  Maud how to spell "fourteen" (the muster line he picked her from).
  Aske makes everyone sit and eat: nobody has eaten since the bell, and
  she will do the deer. Maud starts to say she will write to Greywater
  Abbey and stops: she can't write to the Abbey, not ever again; and
  past the boundary stone they hang the lead for that coat. Rue, paper in
  hand: the seal belongs to a hall, everybody knows where Veyne Hall is,
  she is going there, alone if she has to. The lead: "Not alone." Rue:
  in front of her, then, where she can see them. Hollis gets up off the step, cleans the lead's sword on his
  own sleeve, gives it back, and says nothing about the hall or the boy
  (any of the dead he names with one small true thing each, and buries).
  Down in the hamlet somebody has found the bell rope.
- **Purpose:** the consequences of the fight and the chapter's question
  (why would Dace do this?), turned into the Act 1 goal.
- **Changes:** *information* (the Veyne seal; Dace holds the title; Rue
  has seen the seal before), *relationship* (Rue distrusts the lead;
  Hollis's silence), *plan* (leave for Veyne Hall; the lead is now an
  outlaw).
- **Every path** (0716): the order bears the Veyne seal, only the holder
  of the title seals with it and that is Dace, and they leave for Veyne
  Hall. Without Rue the order is found by narration, the cart is never
  mentioned, and the choice is the lead's alone, about leaving. Without Tamsin nobody
  goes to the body and narration names the title. Without Hollis the
  lead buries the dead alone, and nobody buries Harl. The ledger records
  what each death leaves unsaid.

### 5. To be continued (`ch01_tbc`)

- **Where:** Veyne Hall, that night, the old lord's study (the
  `veyne_study` map, with the `ch01_pacing` opening).
- **Who:** Dace, and a Vigil messenger.
- **What happens:** two letters lie sealed on the desk, written before
  any news came. The news: Harl dead in the toll-fort, the exile walking
  north with five others. The messenger: the Master of Vows asks whether
  it is done, in writing. Dace hands him one of the two letters: "It's
  done." Alone, he locks the other, unopened, in the desk drawer on top
  of the seal, sits in the old lord's chair, which he never does when
  anyone can see, and tells the lead who isn't there that they never
  once did as they were told. "Good."
- **Purpose:** the rival's face and his doubleness (he lies to his own
  masters and is glad), and the Master of Vows named without
  explanation. The player now knows more than the lead does.
- **Changes:** *information for the player*: Dace sent the killers, is
  covering for the lead's survival, and answers to someone in the Vigil.

## Continuity notes

- Harrowby: forty people, a chapel, a mill, a ford. The toll-fort is an
  old ruin at the ford, and the Red Company arrived the night before.
- The lead and Hollis have lived there four years. Maud has been keeper
  for six. Rue arrived this autumn. Tamsin has guarded the hamlet since
  last winter. Aske poaches the woods and sells game to the hamlet.
- Wren is **not mentioned by name** in Chapter 1: Hollis's "one's in the
  ground", once, in the yard.
- The lead's name: `{lead}` token; the family name "Veyne" is a name
  token. Pronoun tokens only (0708). No gendered words ("lad", "lass",
  "my lord", "my lady"). Harl's "Your Grace" is sarcasm and
  gender-neutral; Tamsin's "Your Former Grace" is a kept word
  (`setting-and-tone.md`).
- Everyone's names in dialogue text go through the names table
  ([`../names.md`](../names.md), ticket 0709), so renames stay one-line
  changes.

## Who the king's army is here

The outline's Chapter 6 names no boss. This sheet's boss is a **knight
of the king's vanguard** (`crown_knight`, nameless, a speaker): the
king's own army, not the Hounds, so Dace stays offstage until the cut
(the outline's Dace column is empty for this chapter, and Ch7 shows him
shaken by Crane); the Hounds are the waves that come out to cut the party
off, which is what Dace's "every man" was ordered here for. The knight
was on this plain three years ago and remembers it as the day the war
was won; his grievance is one a player could share (his men died to
Brennish steel for ten years before the fire; he believes the raids).
He shares history with Oriel, who gave the order he cheered. The
**Candles** themselves are never on the map and never on screen: their
line is a row of small fires lighting along the king's front, seen from
the Brennish lines after the crossing (the bible: the taking of children
is shown by its aftermath, never lingered on).

## What the chapter must do (the outline, `../outline.md`)

- **Goal:** reach the Ashfields before the two armies clash, and put the
  envoy's proof in front of the Brennish commander.
- **Conflict:** the plain is full of echoes (fire elementals: old vows
  still burning). The king's army has brought new Candles.
- **Turn:**
  - Oriel Mast, the crown battle-mage who gave the Candles the order
    three years ago, deserts to the party. He confesses that the prince
    rode into the field and burned on the king's own order, and that
    Aurel's body was never found in the ash, "and they looked" (a clue
    for the midpoint twist; it reads as an ordinary war mystery).
  - Rue tells Hollis that Wren walked out of the fire alive.
  - The Brennish commander believes the proof, but the king's army
    attacks anyway. The war is on.
- **Arcs advanced:** Rue and Aske (the register told Aske that Rue was a
  Candle; here she learns Rue stood on the fire line that burned her
  brother's company), Hollis (Wren lives), the king's secret (for the
  player); from the sheets, Oriel (tells the truth; Rue refuses to give
  him what he wants), Hedda (stands at her own army's lines, where she
  could stay, and walks back), Joss (the proof is believed and the war
  comes anyway; he decides it was for nothing; the answer is 1073's),
  Gil (Cairnford: a reason not to go into town, and nothing more said),
  Aske (home is two days over the hills; she doesn't go).
- **Leave for later:** the word "Door" (nobody says it; the commander
  reads the king's order as a king who will burn anything); Wren's
  whereabouts (Rue saw her led away, and no more); Crane (Chapter 7);
  Hollis telling Dace (Chapter 8). Oriel reads the missing body as a war
  mystery and says so; nobody guesses further.

## Where each piece of the turn goes

The outline gives the turn in three parts and no order. This sheet
spreads them over the night, the lines and the dead ground, because the
three scenes before the first move are capped at 28 boxes between them
and the night already has a new face and the recognition to carry:

- **The night (before the battle):** Oriel arrives and is recognized;
  Aske learns which line; Oriel says why he deserted, which is the
  king's order (the prince rode in, he sent a rider, the answer came
  back burn, he gave it). Four boxes at most on the order.
- **The lines (after the battle):** the proof is believed; then the
  horns; then, asked by the commander what the king's war is for, Oriel
  says the body was never found, "and they looked": the clue, said once,
  in front of the Brennish. If Oriel fell in the crossing the clue goes
  unsaid on that path (Classic loses content; the midpoint reveal
  doesn't depend on it).
- **The dead ground (after the lines):** Rue tells Hollis. It waits
  until the plain is behind him because she will not say it while he has
  still to walk across the place.

A rewrite may move the order's confession to the lines and the
recognition alone to the night if the cap allows.

## Scenes

### 1. The north road (`ch06_intro`)

- **Where:** the border country, ten days out of Saltmere, first frost;
  a road climbing toward Cairnford with bare hills ahead and something
  gray on the wind. Plays when the chapter opens (the world map
  appears). Music: `talk_calm`.
- **Who:** the lead, Aske, Hedda, Maud, Hollis, Tamsin, Gil (each in
  an `@if`).
- **What happens:** Aske has stopped in the road with her face up. The
  wind has ash in it, and she says what it is before anyone else knows
  the smell: the plain. Two days. (She doesn't say "home"; the hills
  past it are hers, and the only person who knows that is the player,
  from Chapter 1.) Hedda has her cloak off and around Maud, who is blue
  and arguing about it and losing. Hollis walks beside the lead again,
  not at the back, and his knee is loud in the cold; Tamsin asks about
  the knee instead of the plain, because nobody is going to ask him
  about the plain. Gil (if there) has gone quiet since the road sign and
  announces a rule nobody has heard: a champion doesn't enter a fair town
  without a purse to lay down; he'll wait by the road while they buy. It
  is plainly made up, and Tamsin says so, and he says it's very old, and
  nobody pushes. Hollis: "Walk." (A box of Joss worrying over the paper and
  Rue shoving bread at him was cut at the cold read: the opening read
  as a roll call of pair-ups; Joss has the dawn and the lines.)
- **Purpose:** where they are and what's ahead (the plain, not the
  numbers); Aske not okay without saying why; the people being people
  (a cloak, a knee, a made-up rule).
- **Changes:** *plan* (Cairnford, then the plain), *relationship* (Hedda
  and Maud; Gil's refusal, left alone).

### 2. The Burnt Mill (`ch06_burnt_mill_intro`, `ch06_burnt_mill_victory`)

- **Where:** a mill burned in the war, roofless, on the last water
  before the plain. In the millrace, something moving that is a fire
  with nobody holding it. Guards the path to the plain; the first echoes
  (1071). Music: the scene before, `dungeon_tense`; the battle, the
  `skirmish` pool (the battle file's); after, `talk_calm`.
- **Who:** Rue, Maud, Tamsin, Hollis, Hedda, Aske (each in an `@if`).
- **Before (3–6):** Rue sees it and goes cold and exact: that's an echo;
  somebody's vow, still going, with the somebody dead; it drinks fire,
  so nobody throws fire at it, her included, and she has nothing else,
  and she says so once, angrily. Maud knows the Vigil's word for it and
  starts a prayer and stops. Tamsin: how do you kill a fire; Hollis: you
  don't stand in it; Hedda: "Easy." Not okay: Rue (useless against the
  one thing that is hers). Nobody says the numbers: the battle note does.
- **After (2–4):** it went out like a lamp, and the millrace steams. Rue
  stands too near the warm place. Aske watches her and hands her nothing
  (a rewrite may give Aske the bread this time; this sheet keeps the
  bread for the dead ground). Hollis: eat.
- **Changes:** *information for the player* (what an echo is, said once
  by the one who cares), *the path* (the way to the plain is open).

### 3. Brennish Outriders (`ch06_outriders_intro`, `ch06_outriders_victory`)

- **Where:** a hill road above the plain, Brennish scouts on it: the
  army's riders, who see an Ardevali coat, Hound black and a hill girl
  walking with them. Optional (1071). Music: the scene before, none (the
  world map's stays); after, `talk_calm`.
- **Who:** Hedda, Aske, Joss, Tamsin (each in an `@if`).
- **Before (3–6):** Hedda shouts across who she is, Ragna Holt's own
  shield, with paper for the commander; they don't believe a Ravn of the
  Holt hall walks with southerners and a Hound. One of them looks at
  Aske and says, in their own tongue, the word for a hill girl who walks
  with Ardevali; the narration says that much and spells nothing. Aske
  goes still and literal and tells him her brother's company and where
  it stood, and that she will shoot him if he says it again, which he
  does. Hedda, embarrassed for her country: "Easy. They're frightened.
  So am I." Not okay: Aske (her own people); Hedda.
- **After (2–4):** one yields. Hedda sends him back with her name and
  "coming, with paper" (the commander has heard of her before the lines).
  Aske, to the rider's back: tell them she is not with the Ardevali;
  tell them she is going home. She isn't; she picks up her arrows. Joss
  (if there) asks whether that was the right thing to let him go and
  gets no answer from anyone but Tamsin: "It was a thing. Walk."
- **Changes:** *information* (the commander is warned the party is
  coming; the Brennish believe the raids), *relationship* (Aske and her
  own side).

### 4. The edge of the plain, dusk (`ch06_edge`)

- **Where:** where the grass stops. Ash to the horizon, frost on it at
  the edges and warm patches underfoot, and far apart, left and right,
  two lines of fires: the king's and Brennmark's. Between them lights
  that move. The first of the battle's intro scenes (0852 wires it).
  Music: `graveyard_desert`.
- **Who:** the lead, Tamsin, Hedda, Hollis, Maud, Aske, Rue (each in an
  `@if`).
- **What happens:** Tamsin counts fires out loud, both sides, to fill the
  quiet, and loses count. Hedda stands looking at the right-hand fires
  and says only that those are hers; her face does something and she
  turns it away. Hollis stands looking at the middle of the plain and
  says nothing, and Maud stands behind him and can't go nearer, and then
  does, one step, and he lets her. Aske looks past both lines at the
  hills, and when Hedda asks what she's looking at says "Nothing. Hills."
  Rue sits with her back to all of it and eats, and when the lead looks
  at her: "What? I've seen it." The moving lights are echoes; they walk
  at night. Hollis: first light; nobody goes out there in the dark.
- **Purpose:** the plain as a place, through what each person can't look
  at; the two armies without a count; why they camp.
- **Changes:** *plan* (dawn), *relationship* (Maud a step nearer
  Hollis).

### 5. The night: Oriel (`ch06_eve`)

- **Where:** the same edge, a small fire (Gil carried the mill's charred
  wood; without him, Hollis did), Hedda on watch, everyone else
  pretending to sleep. Plays before the battle, after the edge, before
  the dawn. Music: `scene_sad`.
- **Who:** the lead, Oriel (no `@if` yet: a speaker); Hedda, Hollis, Rue,
  Aske, Maud, Gil (each in an `@if`). Without Hedda, Hollis has the
  watch; without both, the lead does.
- **What happens:** "Easy. Stop there." A man walks in out of the dark
  with his hands open and the badge torn off a crown mage's robe, sober,
  unfed, and asks for whoever is in charge; Hollis stands in front of
  the lead. He gives his name. **Rue knows the voice before she sees
  him**, and says his name, and he says hers back and where she stood,
  because he can't not: third line, fourth from the left. **Aske hears
  "third line."** She asks which line burned the hill companies. He
  tells her plainly: all of them; the third was the center; and the
  order was his. She does not scream. She says the plain thing (that her
  brother's company stood in front of the third line, then; that she
  has wanted to meet the one who gave it; that he is smaller than she
  thought) and that she is going to sit somewhere else, and does. She
  looks at Rue on the way, and Rue says "Yes." Nobody fixes it. The
  lead (a short neutral line) asks why he's here. **Why:** the new
  children are in the king's camp and tomorrow he is to command their
  line, and he won't; and then, because he refuses himself the excuse
  (*Won't say*: "orders"), the first time: the prince's banner rode
  into the field, he sent a rider to the king's tent to ask him to hold
  the fire, the answer came back burn, and he gave the order. Two boxes
  at most. **Choice 1:** the lead
  to Oriel (earnest: what do you want to do now, the question nobody
  has asked him in three years / blunt: you cross with us; then we'll
  see / an act: sit; you're shaking); each moves him. Nobody says he can
  stay and nobody says he can't (the practical part, where he walks and
  who watches him, is Hollis's at dawn). Gil (if there)
  sits down next to him and says nothing (narration). The fire sinks
  and Oriel takes a flint out and swears at it, and nobody stops him.
  **He is with the party.** (Maud's question to him, whether the
  children were fed where they were kept, was cut for the cap; it is a
  support's, 1073.)
- **Purpose:** the recruit and the first half of the turn; Aske's
  hardest scene (the outline), with the lead's reply going to Oriel
  because Aske would not ask the lead anything here; Rue refusing to
  give him what he wants (she says his name and "Yes" and nothing else).
- **Changes:** *the army* (Oriel joins), *information* (the king's order,
  to the party), *relationship* (Aske and Rue: the third line; Aske and
  Oriel; the lead and Oriel).
- **Every path:** without Rue, he recognizes nobody; he says who he is
  and what he commanded, and Aske asks her question of him directly;
  without Aske, nobody asks about the hill companies and he says it to
  nobody: that the third line was the center. Without both, the scene is
  his confession alone.

### 6. Dawn: the crossing (`ch06_prebattle`)

- **Where:** the edge, first light; frost on the ash gone pink; horns
  from the king's side and dust along the plain's left edge where riders
  are coming out to cut the party off from the Brennish lines. Music:
  `dungeon_tense` (the battle's own cue, `battle_war`, is the battle

- **Who:** the lead, Oriel; Joss, Hedda, Hollis, Rue (each in an `@if`).
- **What happens:** Joss knows the horn (it isn't the recall; it's the
  advance; the captain's). Oriel, in the drill voice, quiet: the echoes
  wake as the sun warms the ash; the black ground is black for a reason;
  walk around it, and walk, don't run, the ash is deep. Hedda: the paper

  only thing she says to him all morning: "Don't give anybody orders."
  Him: "No." Hollis: Veyne in the middle. Nobody says how far the lines

- **Purpose:** why this fight (the Hounds are between them and the way
  back; the lines are the only way out) and how to live on the plain
  (the echoes, once, by the man who made them).
- **Changes:** *stakes* (no way back), *plan* (the crossing).

### 8. The lines (`ch06_victory`)

- **Where:** the Brennish front: shield men with spears leveled, hill
  banners, ash to everyone's knees, somebody being sick. The commander,
  a hill-hold man in his forties, tired, comes down the line himself.
  Music: `talk_calm`; at the horns, `scene_tragic`.
- **Who:** the lead, the commander; Hedda, Joss, Oriel, Aske (each in an
  `@if`). Without Hedda, the lead hands the paper over and the commander
  is slower to believe it (the sheet of Ragna: it "holds less well").
- **What happens:** The spears come down when Hedda says her name ("The
  rider said you were coming, Ravn. He said it like a lie."). Hedda
  hands over Ragna's paper, in her hand and three others. He reads it
  twice, standing. Joss stands very straight. **He believes it**: he
  says so plainly, and that he has wanted to for a year, and that it
  changes nothing about where his shield wall stands this morning. Then
  **the horns from the far side**, and along the king's front a row of
  small fires lighting, one after another, left to right: the Candles'
  line (nobody names the children; the commander says what the fires
  are, in one box). "They're coming anyway." The paper goes inside his
  coat. He asks the deserter (if Oriel lives) the one thing: the king's
  son burned here, they say; is that what this is for? **Oriel: the
  prince rode in and the order came back burn; and they never found
  him; two days he walked the ash with the search parties, and they
  looked; they found the horse.** The commander hears a king who burned
  his own son and will burn anything, and says so; no one guesses
  further. **Joss: three times he said it.** Nobody answers him (the
  answer is 1073's). Then the commander to Hedda: you're home, Ravn;
  stand in. She looks at the lead. "I'm lent." And to Aske, as the party
  turns: "You could walk home from here, hill girl." Aske: "I know where
  it is." She picks up her pack. **Hedda walks back to the party.**
- **Purpose:** the outline's turn, second and third parts (believed, and
  it doesn't matter; the body); Hedda's arc beat; Joss's; Aske's,
  unspoken.
- **Changes:** *information* (the proof is in the commander's coat; the
  body was never found), *stakes* (the war is on), *the party* (Hedda
  chose; Aske stayed).

### 9. The dead ground (`ch06_after`)

- **Where:** behind the Brennish camp, that night, the ridge between
  them and the plain lit red from the other side. Hollis sits facing it.
  Rue sits down across from him, which she has never done. Music:
  `scene_sad`.
- **Who:** the lead, Hollis, Rue (each in an `@if`). Without Rue, nobody
  tells him (the ledger, and 0750: Chapter 8's line then can't be "Rue
  saw her"); without Hollis, Rue tells the lead instead, in two boxes,
  because somebody should know.
- **What happens:** Rue: the book's wrong about one thing; she said
  she'd say (Chapter 3). **Wren walked out.** After. Two keepers in gray,
  not the Vigil's brown, took her by the arms and led her toward the
  king's side; she was walking on her own feet; she looked back once.
  Rue didn't go after her. She was running the other way. (That is the
  thing she can't say and says.) **Hollis says the least**: "...Which
  way." "Walking." Then he asks about her hands; Rue: her hands were
  fine; it was Rue's that burned. Hollis, the small story for the living:
  she snored; you'll have heard that; Rue: everybody heard that. **Choice
  2:** the lead to Hollis (earnest: we'll find her / an act: eat
  something, his own words / saying nothing); each moves him in one or

## Continuity notes

- The plain is two days north of the border country's hills; Cairnford
  sits on the road before it. The king's army camps on the plain's south
  side (the party's left, facing north), Brennmark's on the north side.
  The party camps at the plain's southern edge, east of the king's lines,
  and crosses at dawn. The Hounds come out along the plain's edge from
  the king's side; the echoes drift on the black ground in the middle.
- Aske's hold is in the hills past the Brennish lines: "two days" from
  the lines. She never says "home" in this chapter except to the
  outrider ("tell them I am going home"), which is a lie.
- Oriel: forty-one, sober tonight, badge torn off, no fire vow sworn
  since the Ashfields. He names Rue's place on the line: "third line,
  fourth from the left." He says "the order was mine" and never "orders"
  or "no choice". The king's order, as he tells it: the prince's banner
  rode into the field against orders; he sent a rider to the king's tent
  to ask him to hold the fire; the answer came back "burn"; he gave the
  order. The body: two days with the search parties; they found the
  horse. He reads it as a war mystery and says so ("the fire does that
  sometimes").
- Rue says Oriel's name once, "Yes" to Aske once, "Don't give anybody
  orders" once, and otherwise nothing to him in this chapter (her sheet:
  she won't give him what he wants). Her Ch3 line "that book's wrong
  about one thing" is paid off on the dead ground in those words.
- What Rue saw of Wren: after the fire, two keepers in gray (not Vigil
  brown) led her by the arms toward the king's side; she was walking;
  she looked back once. Rue ran the other way. Nothing of where Wren is
  now (the later twist).
- Hollis calls Wren "my girl". He has never heard Oriel's name before
  tonight and does not learn that Wren was on Oriel's line in this
  chapter (Dace says it in the cut, to the player; Oriel believes Wren
  burned and does not say her name to Hollis). A support (1073) may
  spend that.
- Aske and Tor: "my brother's company" and where it stood; she says
  his name once, to Oriel, and he does not repeat it.
- Hedda calls the lead "outlaw" and Aske "hill girl". The commander
  calls her "Ravn". She says "I'm lent" twice in Act 1 now (the quay,
  the lines); the second is a choice dressed as the first.
- Joss's "three times" is the morning after the hold (Chapter 5); he
  says it once here and nobody answers.
- Gil's Cairnford: a rule invented in the road, nobody pushes, nothing
  of the seventh fair said (his secret stays his; Acts 2–3 tell it).
- The commander is nameless (a speaker, like the magistrate): a

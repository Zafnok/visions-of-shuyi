## What the chapter must do (the outline, `../outline.md`)

- **Goal:** leave the Thornmarch by its only bridge, at Kell's Ford, and
  head for Veyne Hall to ask Dace to his face.
- **Conflict:** Kell's Ford is burning. "Brennish raiders" are killing
  villagers while the Ardevali garrison across the river does nothing.
- **Turn:** Aske sees it first: these are not Brennish, Brennish cannot
  afford boots like this. The raiders are **Hounds in Brennish colors**.
  After the battle a crown herald proclaims that Brennmark has broken
  the Ash Peace, and names the lead an outlaw "seen riding with the
  raiders". The war has started, and it's a lie.
- **Arcs advanced:** Aske (her hatred loses its target), Maud (healing
  strangers under fire), the lead and Dace (Dace's Hounds did this).
- **Recruit:** Joss Pellam, the Hound who won't burn the houses, joins
  in the scene after the battle.
- **Leave for later:** that Joss sewed the badges (Chapter 5); Aske's
  news-selling (Chapter 5; here only that she knows the garrison's hours
  too well, and Tamsin notices); Hollis's silence (he hears "the
  captain" from a dying Hound and from Joss and says nothing, as in
  Chapter 1); Dace's doubleness is shown only in the cut (the pay to
  Hendry's mother, the old guardsman sent somewhere quiet).

## Scenes

### 1. A halt in the dark (`ch02_intro`)

- **Where:** a stand of alders by the road an hour above Kell's Ford,
  before dawn, the river loud below. No fire. Music: `talk_calm`.
- **Who:** the lead, Maud, Hollis, Tamsin, Aske, Rue.
- **What happens:** the party has stopped because Maud sat down. She has
  her boot half off and Hollis stops her: she won't get it back on, a
  keeper's feet have never walked a night. She apologizes for being the
  slow one. Hollis's plan is the bridge at the change of the watch, and
  the lead's coat with the crest cut off is a confession to any sergeant
  who looks at it (he wants it turned inside out, or off). Aske knows
  when the watch changes and says it too exactly; Tamsin asks how she
  knows; "I hunt." Tamsin looks at the river bottom in the dark and
  says what forty acres of it would be (her plant); Rue shoves bread at
  the lead and the lead hasn't eaten (Hollis noticed first). **Choice 1:**
  Maud says to leave her a little behind, she'll catch up; the lead's
  reply moves her (nobody is left; a light answer; an act: the lead's
  shoulder). Then a glow downriver where the ford is, and a bell.
- **Purpose:** warmth before the threat; the people's bodies (feet, a
  knee, bread); the plan (the bridge); the coat; Aske's too-good
  knowledge seeded for Chapter 5; Maud not okay and looked after.
- **Changes:** *relationship* (Maud is carried along, and says so),
  *stakes* (the ford is burning).

### 2. The ford's edge (`ch02_prebattle`)

- **Where:** the south end of the village, by the ford, first light.
  Thatch burning on both banks; the bell is the watchtower's, across
  the water. Music: `dungeon_tense`.
- **Who:** the lead, Aske, Tamsin, Hollis, Maud, Rue.
- **What happens:** a dead man in the road in Brennish colors (a
  villager got him). Tamsin has the old anger ready: Brennish, over the
  ford at night, she has seen this. Aske turns the dead man over with
  her foot and looks at his boots: new, nailed, the same on every one of
  them; Brennish cannot afford boots like this. Tamsin: then who. Aske
  doesn't know, and says the badge is new too. Hollis cuts it: people
  are in those houses, the houses first, the road second; Maud stays
  back with the mare. Maud: "No." She is going to the houses, with her
  hands shaking. Rue says what the raiders are doing (lighting roofs and
  walking to the next one, not fighting anyone) and goes forward. Tamsin
  sees the tower: lit, men on it, watching. Hollis to the lead:
  shoulder up.
- **Purpose:** why this fight (people in the houses, nobody else
  coming); the first half of the turn (not Brennish; the garrison
  watching); Maud overrules Hollis for the first time; Tamsin wrong,
  loudly.
- **Changes:** *information* (not Brennish; the tower is watching),
  *plan* (the houses), *relationship* (Maud's No).

### 3. The battle (defeat the lieutenant, save the houses)

- **Map:** 0848's. Houses on both banks, the ford between, the
  watchtower on the far bank by the bridge; Hendry holds the ford's far
  side. Music: `battle_desperate` (0071, Nick).
- **In-battle scenes** (ids as 0848 wires them; the shapes below):
  - `ch02_first_turn` (turn 1, player): Tamsin, one box: she's the
    fastest, she takes the houses, the rest keep the road.
  - `ch02_refusal` (a turn start early in the battle, 0848 picks which;
    drawn over the map): Hendry and Joss, the exchange of the boss with
    the one who shares his history. Hendry, easy and friendly, sends
    Needle to the next house with the torch; Joss offers the barn
    instead, then can't; Hendry's one `!`, then quietly: come and stand
    by me and we'll say you got lost in the smoke. Joss drops the torch
    in the ford and stands in the water. How Joss is on the map (a Hound
    unit who leaves it after this scene, or no unit at all) is 0848's;
    the player never fights him.
  - `ch02_boss_engage` (anyone): put it down and walk off, he has no
    orders about you; "the village", never "Brennish".
  - `ch02_boss_engage_lead`: he knows the coat, the captain's exile; he
    doesn't want to have to explain the lead to anyone; walk off.
  - `ch02_boss_engage_poacher`, 3–4 boxes: he hears her voice. His
    village, at fourteen, hers over the ford at night; "Then you know
    what it is." "I know what it is."
  - `ch02_boss_half`: the patience goes, the friendliness stays: finish
    the houses, don't stand and watch me.
  - `ch02_death_hound_lieutenant`: his pay to his mother, tell the
    captain, he knows where; then Needle: tell him-- no, tell him
    nothing. "The captain" is how the party learns, if they hadn't from
    Joss, whose men these are (the lead knows who captains the Hounds).
  - `ch02_house_keeper` (Maud ends her move at a house): one box to a
    villager under fire; her arc, strangers.
  - Death quotes (Classic) and retreat lines (Casual) for the five, new
    for this chapter, each a different shape, about the living: Hollis
    (the coat, turn it; the kid's left side), Tamsin (the mare's girth;
    the forty acres, "put me under the wrong column"), Aske (the silver
    north; the boots, she was right), Maud (the people in the houses,
    don't let them come out yet; her sleeves), Rue (the others; the
    bread she was keeping). The lead's fall: one narration box; game
    over.
  - No talk conversations in this chapter (no recruiting by talking;
    Joss joins in a scene).

### 4. The herald (`ch02_victory`)

- **Where:** the ford, mid-morning. What stands, stands; smoke. Maud is
  at a doorway with a villager's arm. Music: `scene_tragic`.
- **Who:** the lead, Tamsin, Hollis, Aske, Rue, Maud (each in an `@if`),
  Joss, the herald.
- **What happens:** the garrison comes over the bridge at last, a crown
  herald in front with a paper; the soldiers stop at the bridge's end.
  He reads it to the village, not to the lead: Brennmark has broken the
  Ash Peace at Kell's Ford; the crown will answer; the exile Veyne, seen
  riding with the raiders, is proclaimed outlaw, and whoever harbors
  them with them. He goes back over the bridge. Tamsin shouts at the
  tower that they were in it and it watched. Hollis: don't answer him;
  he stands closer. Then a boy comes up out of the ford, wet to the
  waist, the badge torn off his coat: that paper was in the lieutenant's
  bag a week ago, he carried the bag; was it right to say that? Rue
  watches his hands. Aske: at home they will believe it; in the
  hill-holds they will say we burned a village; her mother will hear
  that we did this. (Her hatred had a target this morning and hasn't
  one now; she doesn't say so.) **Choice 2:** Tamsin tells the lead
  they're an outlaw now, in writing, it has a price; the lead's reply
  moves Tamsin, Hollis or Aske. Maud, from the doorway: she isn't done,
  there are more, somebody bring water.
- **Purpose:** the turn (the war has started and it's a lie, and the
  lead is named in it); Joss as witness; Aske's beat; the party seen by
  the garrison and not arrested, which is its own tell.
- **Changes:** *information* (the proclamation; the paper was written
  before the raid), *stakes* (the lead is a proclaimed outlaw; the
  bridge is closed to them), *relationship* (Aske and the lead stand on
  the same side of a lie).
- **Every path:** the herald reads and the boy speaks on every path;
  without Tamsin, narration gives the lead the price; without Aske,
  nobody says what the hill-holds will hear.

### 5. The boy from the river (`ch02_join`)

- **Where:** the far bank, after dark, once Aske has taken them back
  over the ford under the tower's nose. Joss on a stone with his boots
  off, wringing his socks. Music: `side_quest` (0071: a young
  companion's introduction).
- **Who:** the lead, Joss, Hollis, Tamsin, Aske, Rue, Maud (the five in
  `@if` blocks).
- **What happens:** Hollis puts bread in Joss's hand without a word.
  Tamsin has the name the lieutenant shouted across the ford: Needle; he
  wishes she wouldn't. Aske, cold: he wore her brother's colors to burn
  farmers. Joss: he only carried the bundle of badges; they came done
  (the lie; his hands give it away to Rue, who says nothing yet). The
  Captain Marr didn't know; he wouldn't. Hollis says nothing; "Eat."
  Joss's tin of needles comes out and he mends the lead's cuff while he
  talks (the plant: the one whole coat he means to make, his own, from
  cloth he picked); Rue sees his hands shaking and holds the cuff for
  him. **Choice 3:** the lead's first words to him (he was waiting to be
  told he did right; he transfers his hero-worship on the spot and calls
  the lead "Your Grace", which he had from the herald's paper; Tamsin
  can't get over it, briefly). Joss is with them.
- **Purpose:** Joss liked, so that later chapters can hurt him; the lie
  planted (the badges "came done"); Hollis and "the captain"; the
  party moving on by the ford, not the bridge.
- **Changes:** *the army* (Joss joins), *relationship* (Tamsin's name
  for him; Aske against him; Hollis feeds him).

### 6. To be continued (`ch02_tbc`)

- **Where:** Veyne Hall, that night, the guard-room (not the study: the
  Chapter 1 cut was there). Music: `talk_antagonist`.
- **Who:** Dace, and Ned Tolley, the old Veyne guardsman, a Hound
  sergeant now.
- **What happens:** two papers: Hendry dead at Kell's Ford; Pellam
  missing, went into the river. Dace: Hendry's pay goes to his mother,
  the full year, he knows where she is. The exile: walked off north, the
  Hound boy with them. Dace tells Ned he's going to Greywater: the prior
  wants a sergeant on his walls and it's quiet, and Ned is old. Ned
  starts to call him "boy" and stops. Dace, cold, done: go on.
- **Purpose:** the Hounds are Dace's and Hendry was his; the warmth that
  a later turn needs; Ned set at Greywater for Chapter 3; the player
  knows the lead is walking toward a man who moves old guardsmen out of
  the way.
- **Changes:** *information for the player*: Dace knows the lead walked
  out of Kell's Ford alive, and the Hound sergeant at Greywater is a
  Veyne man.

## Continuity notes

- Kell's Ford: a village on both banks of the river at the Thornmarch's
  edge, split by its own ford; the crown's bridge a little downstream,
  with the garrison's watchtower on the north bank. The party arrives
  by the road down the south bank, at first light, having walked from
  Harrowby since dusk with no sleep.
- Nobody says how many houses there are, how many men, or how many
  stand: the map and the objective show it.
- The raiders are Hounds in Brennish colors. On screen they are called
  "Brennish" only by Tamsin (wrong) and the herald (lying); Hendry says
  "the village" and "the job"; Joss says "the company" and "the
  lieutenant". Dace is named as their captain twice: by Hendry to the
  lead if they fight ("Dace's exile"), and by Joss on the far bank
  ("Captain Marr didn't know"), with Hollis listening. Hollis never says
  the name, and nobody says "Hounds".
- The herald's proclamation was written and sealed a week before the
  raid, all but the one line that names the exile (Joss saw the space).
  Who added the line is not said.
- Joss's badge: torn off, the gap stitched later (told, not drawn). The
  badges "came done" is his lie; he sewed forty (his sheet; Chapter 5).
- The party crosses by the ford after dark, not by the bridge: the
  garrison has read them a proclamation and gone back to its tower, and
  nobody on it looks down.
- Hollis never says the boy's name (Chapter 1's rule) and says nothing
  about the Hounds being his son's.
- The lead's name: `{lead}`; "Veyne" and every other name is a token.
  Pronoun tokens only. No gendered words. Joss's "Your Grace" and
  Tamsin's "Your Former Grace" are gender-neutral; Hendry's "the
  captain's exile" too.
- The plain build has no portrait for anyone but the lead, so the script
  uses only the five expressions every character has.

## What the chapter must do (the outline, `../outline.md`)

- **Goal:** find out why the Vigil wanted Rue. Maud knows that every
  gifted child's name, and where they were sent, is written in the
  register at Greywater Abbey.
- **Conflict:** the abbey is on a lake island. Its prior is burning
  records under Hound guard, and taking the register makes Maud an
  apostate.
- **Turn:** the register holds Rue's name twice (Vosse's cart; a Candle),
  Wren's name ("Ashfields: lost"), and Vosse's pay-book ("D. Marr,
  informer: eleven children"). Rue tells the lead what she saw at the
  mill, and realizes the lead didn't know. Maud confesses to Rue that her
  report brought Harl. Hollis says nothing, which Rue notices.
- **Arcs advanced:** Maud (breaks with the Vigil), Rue (tells), Hollis
  (his silence starts to show), the lead and Dace (the truth, on paper);
  from the sheets, Aske (the register says Rue was a Candle; she stops
  speaking to her) and Joss ("it must be another Marr").
- **Leave for later:** Hollis's secret (Chapter 4: Dace says "You knew");
  Wren alive (Chapter 6; here only that the register says *lost* where the
  family was told *dead*, and Rue says the book is wrong about one thing,
  "not tonight"); the Door (Chapter 7); Dace face to face (Chapter 4).

## The prior dies

Nick (0743, 2026-10-28): the prior "can say dies or surrenders"; this
ticket picks **dies**. The outline's table gives Chapter 3 only the

## Scenes

### 1. The boathouse (`ch03_intro`)

- **Where:** a boathouse on the lake shore, the night before, a small
  fire inside it. Across the water the island, the abbey's walls, and one
  chimney smoking against the dark. Music: `talk_calm`.
- **Who:** the lead, Maud, Rue, Aske, Joss, Tamsin, Hollis.
- **What happens:** Rue has the fire hogged and is burning the porridge;
  Aske takes the pot off her. Maud has not eaten; she is looking at the
  chimney. Nobody burns at night but somebody burning what they
  shouldn't, and she knows the room: the long one over the gate, where
  the register is kept, and she knows what's in it because she has
  written in it (said once, plainly, by the person it costs). Rue wants
  to go tonight, in a boat: the book says where the others were sent.
  Aske is learning letters with Maud by the fire, with a stick in the
  ash, badly, and goes on anyway (the plant for the scriptorium). Tamsin
  is wrong: they're priests, they'll open the gate to a keeper and a
  civil word. Aske counts the men on the wall: black, not hoods. Joss:
  that's company black, and the company hangs deserters, and his face is
  known; he says it too much and apologizes for saying it, and then
  mends somebody's sleeve. Hollis: "My girl's in that book", one sideways
  true thing, then an order to eat. **Choice 1:** Hollis asks the lead
  what they want out of the book; the reply moves him (to "Dace's name"
  he gives an order about food: the silence, showing). The chimney goes
  all night.
- **Purpose:** warmth before the threat (porridge, letters, a sleeve); the
  register said once by Maud; the Hounds on the wall said once by Joss;
  Maud and Joss not okay; Tamsin wrong; Hollis's silence seeded.
- **Changes:** *information* (the men on the walls are Hounds, not
  keepers), *plan* (the long room over the gate), *relationship* (Hollis
  asks the lead a question he can't answer himself).

### 2. The causeway (`ch03_prebattle`)

- **Where:** the foot of the causeway at dawn: a mile of stone over gray
  water, the gate at the end, black on the walls, the chimney still
  going. Music: `dungeon_tense`.
- **Who:** the lead, Maud, Hollis, Joss, Rue, Tamsin, Aske.
- **What happens:** Maud is already three steps out with her hood up:
  nobody looses at a keeper's hood, she'll go first. Hollis: the ones on
  the wall aren't keepers, she stood in a
  village with them. She knows; she was wrong, and says so. Joss does
  the drill under his breath (two on the walk, one at the bell, the bar
  goes down when we're close enough to count) and puts his hood up: his
  face is known. Tamsin hates the causeway (one way on, one way off,
  nowhere to turn the mare) and tells Maud, gray in the face, to get up
  behind her. Aske: they aren't loosing; they're letting us come closer.
  Hollis: then closer; the kid behind him to the gate. The bar goes
  down. (Rue has no line here: a roll call of one observation each was
  what the cold reads skipped, and she carries the night before and the
  two scenes after.)
- **Purpose:** why this fight (the names are going up the chimney and
  nobody else is coming); the one road; Maud's last illusion about the
  Vigil gone before the gate; Joss scared and drilling; no quips.
- **Changes:** *information* (they've been seen; the room), *plan* (the
  causeway, Hollis first).

### 4. The scriptorium (`ch03_register`)

- **Where:** the scriptorium, right after: the hearth full of half-burned
  red ledgers, the big register open on the desk where he left it, soot
  on everything. Music: `scene_tragic`.
- **Who:** the lead, Maud, Tamsin, Rue, Aske, Hollis, Joss (each in an
  `@if`).
- **What happens:** Tamsin pulled ledgers out of the hearth with her
  sleeves over her hands and Maud sees to the hands before anything
  (nobody says thank you). Maud takes the register off the desk and says
  once, plainly, what that makes her, and can't look: Rue looks. Rue
  finds her own name and reads the cart entry flat, then a second one
  (caught a year after), and skips one word of it reading aloud. Aske,
  who has had two nights of letters, reads the word herself: Candle, the
  word for the ones who burned the plain; "You were one of them." "Yes."
  Aske takes her finger off the page, goes out, and says nothing else to
  Rue in the chapter. Hollis: Keeper, find my girl; you wrote her in,
  you'll know the page. The first words of Wren's line are in Maud's own
  hand and she says so, and reads the rest: sworn to fire, the year, the
  Ashfields, lost. Hollis: they told us dead; lost. Tamsin finds a second
  book in the drawer: a paymaster's columns (she has stood in front of
  enough of those with her hand out) and a name on the cover, which
  Hollis places, since the player has never heard it: the man from the
  mill. Tamsin reads, slowly: D. Marr, informer, eleven, children. Joss:
  there are hundreds of Marrs, every third farm in the valley, his ma's
  cousin married one, it's another Marr. Hollis has gone to the window.
  Rue isn't looking at the book; she's looking at Hollis. The lead
  hasn't moved from the door.
- **Purpose:** the turn on paper, in front of everyone; Maud's break;
  Aske's beat; Wren's "lost"; Hollis's silence seen by Rue; Joss
  defending the captain.
- **Changes:** *information* (Rue was a Candle; Wren is "lost"; D. Marr
  took pay for eleven children), *stakes* (Maud is an apostate; the
  register is the party's), *relationship* (Aske and Rue; Hollis and Maud
  thaw by one line; Rue watching Hollis).
- **Every path:** the register is read on every path (narration shows the
  pages where nobody alive can read them aloud); who takes it is Maud,
  else Rue, else the lead; the pay-book is found by Tamsin, else Joss,
  else narration.

### 5. The causeway at night (`ch03_telling`)

- **Where:** the end of the causeway, that night, Rue with her boots on
  and her feet over the water, bread in her lap. Hollis a shape walking
  the wall above. Music: `scene_sad`. (The skill's "ask me after" became
  "I've said enough for one night": "after" on its own is British.)
- **Who:** the lead, Rue (Hollis in narration, in his `@if`). The whole
  scene is in Rue's block: without her it isn't played.
- **What happens:** Rue gives the lead half the bread without a word (the
  habit; no counting this time). Then she tells it: fifteen, the cart,
  the boy who lifted her in by the belt like a sack and was crying and
  did it anyway, the blue wax on the pass nailed to the cart, the man
  with the lantern saying the name; then the boy found his own sister
  in the cart and killed the lantern man and the children ran. She has
  walked in front for two chapters because she thought the lead knew.
  **Choice 2:** the lead's reply; she sees the lead didn't know, and the
  grudge she's carried has nowhere to go. Then: the book is wrong about
  one thing; not tonight (Wren, unsaid). Then: and the old man said
  nothing, not one word, he says Eat to everybody and he said nothing;
  she watched him; did the lead?
- **Purpose:** Rue tells (her arc); the lead's innocence, seen by the one
  person who doubted it; Hollis's silence named; Wren hooked for Chapter
  6.
- **Changes:** *information* (what Dace did at the cart, from a witness),
  *relationship* (the lead and Rue: the suspicion is over and nothing has
  replaced it yet; she drops "heir" once).

### 6. The kitchen (`ch03_confession`)

- **Where:** the abbey kitchen, later: a hearth that works, the novices'
  porridge pot, Rue has eaten everything. Music: `scene_sad` (unchanged).
- **Who:** Maud, Rue, the lead. In both blocks: without either, it isn't
  played.
- **What happens:** Maud comes in with the salve she has put on Rue's
  hands every night since Harrowby and can't start; she fails twice,
  apologizes for failing, and says the plain thing: the paper on the
  chapel door said "the girl with the burned hands" because those were
  Maud's words, written to the Abbey in the autumn, asking them to be
  gentle with her. Rue: short boxes; one of only "...". "Give me the
  salve." Then, to the lead: you heard her, well? **Choice 3:** the reply
  moves Rue or Maud (one reply is an act: the lead takes the salve and
  does Rue's hands). Rue, last: she isn't going anywhere, where would
  she go; she'll do her own hands tonight; Keeper. Maud sits down on the
  hearth step.
- **Purpose:** Maud confesses (her arc); the one hurting most says the
  least; nobody forgives anybody yet.
- **Changes:** *information* (Rue knows who reported her), *relationship*
  (Maud and Rue crack; the lead stood in the room).

## Continuity notes

- Greywater Abbey: the Vigil's house for the Thornmarch, on a lake
  island two days north of Kell's Ford, reached by a stone causeway; the
  scriptorium is the long room over the gate, with the chimney; the
  reliquary is another room (the chests). The party came the long way
  around the lake to it, off the road to Veyne Hall.
- Nobody says how many men are on the walls, how long the causeway is in
  tiles, how many chests or how many turns: the map and the objective
  show it.
- The register's entries, as the script shows them: Rue (a
  charcoal-burner's daughter of the Thornmarch; taken by the cart;
  escaped) and a second entry (sworn to fire; Candle; the Ashfields:
  deserted); Wren Marr (Harrowby; tested, in Maud's hand; sworn to fire;
  the Ashfields: lost). The pay-book is Vosse's own, kept at Greywater
  with his effects: "D. Marr, informer: eleven children". Nothing in the
  register says who reported Rue this autumn (the prior sent Maud's
  letter on to Ossary; it is not in the children's register), so Maud's
  confession is still hers to make.
- Rue knows Wren walked out of the fire and says only that the book is
  wrong about one thing, "ask me after". Hollis hears *lost* and not
  *dead* and no more.
- "D. Marr" is read aloud as it is written; nobody says "Dace" over the
  book. Joss says it must be another Marr; Hollis says nothing; Rue
  tells the lead that night who it was.
- Aske hears "Candle" by Rue's name and stops speaking to her. She
  doesn't learn here that Rue stood on the line that burned her
  brother's company (Chapter 6).
- The prior is dead; Ned Tolley dies if the player kills him (the script
  doesn't know, so the cut to Veyne Hall doesn't mention him).
- Hollis never says the boy's name (Chapter 1's rule). Ned says "the
  boy", as Hollis does.
- The lead's name: `{lead}`; "Veyne" and every other name is a token.
  Pronoun tokens only. No gendered words.
- The plain build has no portrait for anyone but the lead, so the script
  uses only the five expressions every character has; the prior and Ned
  have no `happy`.

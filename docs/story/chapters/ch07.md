## What the party knows walking in

From the ledger (end of Chapter 6): the Candles are kept in a barracks
under Ossary (Rue lived in it; she has said "the barracks" since
Chapter 1); nineteen new Candles were lit along the king's front at the
Ashfields; Wren walked off the plain alive with two keepers in gray,
toward the king's side (Hollis and the lead know; nobody else); Maud's
autumn report went up to Ossary (Rue, the lead and Maud know it brought
Harl); Dace answers to Ossary; **nobody in the party has heard Crane's
name or the word "Door"**, and Maud has never said who ordained her.
The party has no reason left to be in the war: the paper is in a
Brennish coat, believed and useless. What is left is the children, and
Wren, and Maud's names.

## Who Crane is to them

Maud's old master (the outline's conflict): the Master of Vows ordained
her, and she says so on the road, which is how the party first hears
his name. To Oriel, the man who handed him the children and once
corrected him for using a name. To Rue, the man who read about her
hands. To Hollis, the man his son writes to. To the lead, a man who has
read their confession. He never says a child's name; the Candles are
"the candles". He is never angry and never hurries. Hobb steps between
him and Rue's hands because Rue would burn him and because this is her
room, and that is why he walks out of it.

## Where the Candles went (this ticket's call, from the ledger)

The dormitory is empty because its children went to the war: a
fortnight before the party arrives they were carted to the king's army,
and they are the nineteen whose fire line lit along the king's front at
the Ashfields (Chapter 6). Crane says so in one line; Oriel, who was to
have commanded them that morning, hears it. Hobb was told they were
"going home" and chose to believe it. Wren is not among them and is not
here: Crane calls her "catalogued" and says the register's word, lost,
was Greywater's, and no more. Nothing of where she is (the later
twist), and Dace is told nothing (Chapter 8's line is Hollis's).

## What the chapter must do (the outline, `../outline.md`)

- **Goal:** find the surviving Candles, and Wren, in the Vigil's holy
  city.
- **Conflict:** the city is full of pilgrims and wardens, and Maud's old
  master is there.
- **Turn:** the Candles' dormitory under the city is empty. Crane is
  waiting. He calls death "a door", and tells Maud her report was
  "admirably thorough". The party learns that the Door was opened once
  before, in the East, and that the Jade Reach sealed it. Only the East
  knows how to shut it. Crane escapes.
- **Arcs advanced:** Maud (faces Crane), Rue (the children), Dace (seen
  briefly, shaken by Crane); from the sheets, Hollis (an empty cot, and
  "not lost"), Oriel (the room he knew; the nineteen were his), Aske
  (sees the cots), Hedda (out of her depth and says so), Joss (the
  captain's banner in a church city).
- **Leave for later:** where Wren is (the later twist); the prince
  (nobody connects a door to the king's son; Crane's war is "a great
  many deaths", said once); the king's name in Crane's mouth (he says
  "the crown" and no more); Rue's hideout (1072); Maud's letters to the
  families (1072; she says the intent to Crane in one box); Hollis
  telling Dace (Chapter 8); Dace learning Wren lives (Chapter 8).

## Scenes

### 1. The pilgrim road (`ch07_intro`)

- **Where:** a ridge road a week south of the plain, early winter, with
  pilgrims on it: families with bundles, a few barefoot, walking the
  same way. From the ridge, Ossary for the first time: towers, and
  bells, many bells, and one cracked. Plays when the chapter opens (the
  world map appears). Music: `holy_city` (0853 adds it).
- **Who:** the lead, Maud, Hedda, Rue, Hollis, Tamsin, Gil, Aske (each
  in an `@if`).
- **What happens:** Hedda has never heard so many bells in her life and
  says so; she asks what you do, whether you bow, whether you pay; she
  is out of her depth and says so plainly. Maud knows the bells by name
  and names the cracked one (the small chapel's; she was ordained under
  it) and then, because she can't not, says who ordained her: the
  Master of Vows, Absalom Crane, who is there, because it's winter and
  he doesn't travel. (The party hears his name for the first time.
  Nobody makes anything of it yet; Rue goes still.) Tamsin: the
  pilgrims are carrying bundles and some of the bundles are bones;
  they're selling bones at the gate; who buys a bone? Maud: people
  whose own are lost; you buy one and bury it and it's him. Gil (if
  there) has a rule about holy ground, number six, no blows struck on
  it, and has been worrying about it since the first bell; Tamsin
  tells him the cellar isn't the church; he isn't sure a cellar under a
  church isn't the church. Rue, asked by nobody, says the way in: the
  Candles' door is a laundry yard behind the ossuary and a kitchen
  stair that's never locked in the day because of the steam; the
  barracks are at the bottom. Aske counts hoods and says the number is
  high, and nothing else. Hollis: in and out; the keeper in front,
  hood down; nobody draws steel under a roof unless he says.
- **Purpose:** the plan said once, by the one who knows it; the city as
  a place through what each person can't take (bells, bones, hoods);
  Crane's name planted; Maud not okay without saying why.
- **Changes:** *plan* (the kitchen stair), *information* (the Master of
  Vows ordained Maud, and is in the city).

### 2. Pilgrims' Road (`ch07_pilgrims_road_intro`, `ch07_pilgrims_road_victory`)

- **Where:** a wayside shrine where the road narrows between walls, a
  warden post at it, and the pilgrims in a line with their hands held
  out. Guards the path into Ossary (1071). Music: the scene before,
  `dungeon_tense`; the battle, the `skirmish` pool (the battle file's);
  after, `talk_calm`.
- **Who:** Rue, Maud, Hollis, Tamsin, Hedda (each in an `@if`).
- **Before (3–6):** the wardens are looking at the pilgrims' hands,
  palms up, one at a time, and waving them through. Maud says they
  never did that, hands, why would they look at-- and stops. Rue says
  it for her: they're looking for her. She goes forward before anyone
  can stop her. Hollis: then it's now, before one of them runs for the
  gate. Hedda: "Easy."
- **After (2–4):** the pilgrims have scattered into the walls and
  watch. Maud kneels by a dead warden and says the words over him
  (he was the Vigil's, so somebody has to), and Rue asks whether she's
  really saying words over him, and Maud says yes and that Rue can
  stand there and hate it. Hollis: word reaches the gate by dark, so
  the gate by dark.
- **Changes:** *stakes* (the Vigil is looking for Rue by her hands;
  the city will know they're coming), *relationship* (Maud keeps a vow
  in front of Rue, to a man who'd have taken her).

### 3. The Ossuary Gate (`ch07_ossuary_gate_intro`, `ch07_ossuary_gate_victory`)

- **Where:** outside the city wall, below the ossuary: a low gate in
  the hill where the dead go in and the carts stood. Wardens at it.
  Optional (1071). Music: the scene before, `dungeon_tense`; after,
  `scene_sad`.
- **Who:** Rue, Oriel, Hollis, Tamsin (each in an `@if`).
- **Before (3–5):** Rue stops. This is the gate: they were put in the

  only that. Hollis: it's held, and they don't need this gate. Rue:
  she needs it, and goes forward. Tamsin: then they take it, and will
  Sparks wait for the rest of them, for once.
- **After (2–4):** the gate open and the stair going down into the
  dark. Rue stands in the gate and doesn't go in, and says she's not
  scared of a cellar. Hollis: he knows; not this way, the front way,
  in daylight, with the keeper; walk. (What clearing the gate does for

- **Changes:** *information* (where the carts loaded), *relationship*
  (Hollis and Rue: "I know").

### 4. The streets (`ch07_city`)

- **Who:** Maud, Rue, Hollis (each in an `@if`; the first two in

- **What happens (3):** narration: the market, the kneeling pilgrims,

- **Changes:** *plan* (down).

### 5. The dormitory (`ch07_dormitory`)

- **Where:** down the kitchen stair, warm from the ovens, then cold:
  a long low room with two rows of cots and a blanket folded at the
  foot of each one, aired, and nobody in it. A slate with letters on
  it. Plays after the streets, before Crane. Music: `scene_dread`
  (0853 adds it).
- **Who:** the lead, Rue, Hollis, Oriel, Aske (each in an `@if`).
- **What happens (8):** Rue walks the row and stops. Hers. The next
  one: Wren's; she was always on Rue's left; she said Rue was warm.
  Hollis puts his hand on that folded blanket and it is a child's size,
  and he takes his hand off it (narration, nothing explained). Oriel: he
  came down this stair with the roll and read it out and they stood up,
    all at once; he'd forgotten how low the ceiling is; he used to stoop
  without thinking and has just done it again. Aske: how old. Rue: twelve; the little ones ten.
      Aske: her brother was twenty-two. The scene cuts there, and the next

- **Purpose:** the goal, found empty, through four people who can't
  look at it the same way; Aske one notch (her brother was a man; these

- **Changes:** *information* (nobody's here; somebody still airs the

### 6. Crane (`ch07_crane`)

- **Where:** the same room; a lamp at its far end that nobody saw lit,
  and an old man sitting on the last cot with his hands folded on a
  stick, in a tall hood. Plays straight after the dormitory. Music:
  `talk_antagonist`.
- **Who:** the lead, Crane, Hobb (speakers; no `@if`); Oriel, Maud,
  Hollis, Rue (each in an `@if`).
- **What happens (12–14):** "You'll be looking for the candles." They
  went to the war a fortnight ago; nineteen; the party will have seen
  their fires from the wrong side (Oriel, if there: he was to have had
  them in the morning; Crane: and he wasn't there; that reached him).
    To Maud, by name: her report was **admirably thorough**: the hands,
  the age, what the girl ate; he had only to send a man. Maud: Master,
  she asked him to be gentle with her; it's on the second page; she
  asked in writing. He doesn't answer that. Hollis asks straight: Marr,
  Wren Marr, three years back, where. Crane: Captain Marr; he doesn't
    keep them by name; he keeps them catalogued; nothing of his is
  **lost**; the abbey's book was careless. Without Hollis, the lead
  asks ("Wren Marr. Where is she.") and gets the same answer. When Rue
  is not in the room (she fell before she could tell it on the dead
  ground, or told it there and fell since; an `@if` can't tell which),
  Crane adds how she left the field: Marr's girl walked off on her own
  feet, between two keepers in gray, and was entered in his book that
  evening; they've mislaid the one with the burned hands; she saw it.
  Her first telling on the one path, a confirmation on the
  other, and true on both (0753). (A first draft had him
  quote Dace's letter about "the old man who wasn't at the gate"; the
  cold read couldn't parse it and counted it as the third "I've read
    your paper" in a row, so it went.) Maud asks what they're for. Crane:
  **death is a door**; she was taught that in her second year. Maud:
  don't; they were taught that Othe kept the Door, that somebody opened
  it once in the East, that the Jade Reach shut it, that the vow was
  buried, and there they stop reading. (Two cold reads found a quiz
  here boring, Crane reciting and then Maud reciting; now she says it
  back at him as a refusal.) Crane: you stop; he didn't; the East opened
  it once and lost its nerve; a war buys a great many deaths; the
  candles who went ahead are on the far side of a door; a door opens.
  Then the heir of
  Veyne: he has read the confession, four pages, a magistrate's copy;
  it was very well done and Dace has put it to good use. **Choice 1:**
  the lead to Crane (earnest: why children / blunt: you sent Harl to
  Harrowby / wry: do you charge for the lesson); each gets a mild,
  exact answer (why: because they were given to him and because they
    burn; a kinder answer, he's looked, there isn't one; Harl: he sent a
  letter, and the Red Company were Dace's choice, he'd have sent
  wardens; does it help, knowing which; the lesson: the crown pays him,
  and well; one never quite stops teaching, and he has a long road).
  Then Rue's hands come up, and the fire with them, and the old man
  doesn't move, and **Hobb** steps out of the dark between them, in
    blue, keys at her belt: "There, now. Not in here, dear. I did the
  blankets this morning." Rue's hands come down. Crane goes out by the
  far door without hurrying and takes the lamp; boots on the kitchen
  stair behind them (narration).
- **Purpose:** the outline's turn, whole; Maud's beat (she faces him and
  it costs her the second page); Hollis's "not lost"; the lead's one
  word to the man who sent the letter; why he walks out alive (Rue
  won't burn Hobb).
- **Changes:** *information* (the candles are at the war; Wren is
  catalogued, not lost; the Door, what it is and that the East shut it
  once; Crane means to open it), *stakes* (wardens on both stairs),
  *relationship* (Maud and Crane: faced).
- **Every path:** without Oriel, Crane says where the candles went and
  nobody answers; without Maud, Crane tells the party what the Vigil
  teaches unasked (he can't help teaching) and nobody is called
  thorough; without Hollis, the lead asks about Wren and hears "lost";
  without Rue, Crane says how Wren left the field, nobody's hands come
  up, and Hobb steps in anyway, to see him out.

### 10. The night after (`ch07_after`)

- **Where:** a pilgrims' barn on the road out, that night, the city's
  bells still going faintly behind them. Maud with her sleeves full of
  salve and nobody's hands to put it on; the lead; Rue with bread.
  Music: `scene_sad`.
- **Who:** the lead, Maud, Rue, Hedda, Hollis (each in an `@if`).
  Without Maud the scene is Rue's and Hedda's and short; without Rue
  nobody says it was the hands.
- **What happens (8–12):** Maud reaches for the lead's hands and stops
  herself: theirs are fine, she looked on the stairs; hers won't keep
  still, look at them. Rue sits down with half a loaf and tears it: he said
  thorough. Maud: yes; two and a half pages; she put in what Rue ate,
  that she ate standing up by the door like she'd have to run; she
  thought it would make them kind. Rue: it was the hands; one line and
    he'd have known; she could have written one line; she's saying it
  wasn't the pages. Maud: "...Oh." Half the bread in her lap.
  **Choice 2:** the lead to Maud (earnest: you said it to his face /
  wry: two and a half pages about her cooking? / act: sit down, give me
  your hands); each moves her (she had a whole speech and said the
  second page, and she did say it to his face; the cooking was one line
  and Rue can't cook, and Rue says she can; her hands won't stop, she
  did say, and she sits). Then the one thing the party takes: Maud: he
  said the East shut it; if they shut it once, somebody there knows
  how. Hedda: ships go east from Saltmere, and Ragna could get them on
  one; she's only saying where ships go. Rue to Maud: eat that first.
  Maud: she's eating. (Without Maud: Rue, Hedda, and Hollis saying
  nobody's going anywhere tonight.)
- **Purpose:** Maud's beat paid (the second page, and what it cost);
  Rue one notch toward her, in Rue's way; the East said once, as a fact
  somebody cares about; people being people.
- **Changes:** *relationship* (Rue and Maud; the lead and Maud),
  *information* (the East knows how it was shut; ships go east).

### 11. To be continued (`ch07_tbc`)

- **Where:** the Vigil's gate, the same hour, a closed carriage, Hounds
  mounted, Dace holding the carriage door. Crane comes out of the
  chapter house without hurrying. Music: `talk_antagonist`.
- **Who:** Crane, Dace.
- **What happens (4–6):** Dace: and Hobb? Crane: holding the stair.

  it's a door; it's what he was promised. Dace, cold: he was promised

## Continuity notes

- Ossary is a week or more south of the plain by the pilgrim road; the
  party came off the Ashfields behind the Brennish lines and walked
  back into Ardeval by the hills (the world map's business; nobody says
  the route). Early winter.
- The Candles' barracks: under the ossuary, down a kitchen stair from a
  laundry yard behind it; a long low room, two rows of cots, a press
  for the blankets, a slate; the wardens' own barracks further in; a
  far stair up to the chapter house. The ossuary gate outside the wall
  is where the carts loaded, out of the pilgrims' sight. The map is
  0853's; nobody says how many stairs or wardens.
- Hobb: fifty-two, keys on a ring at her belt, one for every door; she
  aired the blankets that morning; calls everyone "dear" and Rue by her
  name ("love" and "now then" were her sheet's words; they are British,
  so the script says "dear" and "there, now", and the sheet was
  changed); never says "dead" ("gone ahead", "home"). She speaks twice
  before the fight, so 0853 gives her a face (her sheet: "a face only
  if she speaks outside the fight").
- Crane: sixty-seven; tall hood; a stick; never says a child's name
  ("the candles", lower case in his mouth: the script writes
  `{n:group.candles}`, which the player reads the same); never angry;
  explains like a tutor. He knows Hollis as Captain Marr, Maud by name,
  Dace by his first name, and has read the lead's confession. He says
  "the crown" and never the king's name, "a war" and never "the Door
  Vow". What is said of the Door: Maud recites what novices are taught
  (Othe kept it; somebody opened it once in the East before the gods
  died; the Jade Reach shut it; the vow was buried) and Crane finishes
  it: death is a door; he means to open it. What he says of Wren:
    catalogued; not lost; the abbey's book was careless.
- Where the candles went: carted to the king's army a fortnight before;
  the nineteen at the Ashfields. Said by Crane, heard by Oriel.
- Maud: the Master of Vows ordained her in the small chapel with the
  cracked bell; her report was two and a half pages; she asked them to
  be gentle on the second page; she says to Crane that she'll write to
  the families in the register, in her own hand (*Unwritten*'s intent,
  1072's quest).
- Rue: her cot and Wren's next to it; the warden fed her and locked the
  door; Rue gave the little ones her bread. She never says "please" and
  never says Hobb is dead; she says she couldn't count.
- Hollis: "my girl"; he asks Crane one question and hears "not lost"
  (and, if Rue is gone, how she left the field; he says nothing to it);
  he puts a hand on a child's blanket and takes it off; his death line
  starts a message to "the boy" and stops.
- Aske and Rue are not speaking (Chapter 6). This chapter: "How old."
  "Twelve." Nothing more. Aske calls the city "their church".
- Hedda: "outlaw", "hill girl"; out of her depth with bells and bowing;
  "I'm only saying where ships go."
- Joss: sees the captain's banner in the square and at the gate; wants
  to be told it's nothing.
- Gil: rule six, no blows struck on holy ground; a cellar under a church
  worries him; in the dark, no rules.
- Dace: at the Vigil's gate with Hounds and a carriage; pale; "mount
  up". He has never heard Crane say "door" before tonight and is told
  nothing of Wren.
- Nobody says "Door Vow", the king's name, or the prince. The word
  "Door" is said by Crane and repeated by Maud; the script writes
  `{n:term.door}`.
- The lead's name: `{lead}`; "Veyne" and every other name is a token.
  Pronoun tokens only. No gendered words.
- The plain build has no portrait for anyone but the lead, so the script
  uses only the five expressions every character has.

## What the party knows walking in

From the ledger (end of Chapter 7): the East shut the Door once, so
somebody there knows how; ships go east from Saltmere and Ragna could
get them on one (Hedda said it in the barn); Crane means to open the
Door with a war's deaths; Wren is "catalogued, not lost" (Hollis and the
lead heard it; Hollis knows from Rue that she walked off the Ashfields
alive, toward the king's side, and has told nobody but the lead); Crane
left Ossary with Dace and the Hounds. Nobody has decided to go east.
The king has been at war with Brennmark since the Ashfields; the party
is outlawed since Kell's Ford. The party is on the road south out of
Ossary, early winter, with Ossary's bells behind it.

## Why Saltmere, and why Dace is there

Ragna went by ship's boat to a Brennish ship in the roads at the end of
Chapter 5, and told Hedda she would hold the roads off Saltmere "till
the ice" (said here by Hedda, not shown before: it is the kind of thing
Ragna says and Hedda believes). With the war on, a Brennish ship can
still lie off a neutral League port, and the League sells its dock to
anyone: the dock men have gone home all at once this morning because
someone paid them to, and Hound black is on the fish steps since dawn.
Dace is in Saltmere because the king's bill names the exile a traitor
and the Hounds are to deliver them, and because the exile's people went
to the Brennish envoy once already (Chapter 5). The ship is going east;
Ragna sails with it as far as the Brennish coast, where it puts her off
(she has a hall to reach and a son of eight); Hedda is expected to go
home with her and doesn't.

## Who the king is to the player

Named since Chapter 1, declared the war in Chapter 2, gave the order
that burned the Ashfields (Oriel, Chapter 6): never seen until the last
scene. He reads a boy's adventure book aloud to someone the camera
never shows, in his private rooms at Varenhall, with the lamp lit, and
is gentle. He never says a name. The clue is the outline's (the
midpoint twist; subtle, so the fallback breaks nothing): someone is in
the room, someone he has read to for years, and tonight their hands are
warm. Nothing else is said.

## What the chapter must do (the outline, `../outline.md`)

- **Goal:** get out. The king has declared the lead a traitor and gone
  to war. Ragna has a Brennish ship waiting at Saltmere to take the
  party east, to the only people who know how to shut the Door.
- **Conflict:** Dace and the Hounds are on the docks.
- **Turn:** Hollis dies. He holds the gangway so the party can board.
  Dace orders the archers to stop, too late. Hollis dies between his son
  and the lead, and his last words to Dace are the one thing Dace
  doesn't know: his sister's alive, Rue saw her walk out of the fire, go
  and get her. The ship sails. Dace stands on the dock alone.
- **Classic permadeath:** if Hollis already fell, the lead tells Dace
  instead.
- **Closing scene:** Varenhall, the king's private rooms, lamplight. The
  king reads aloud from a boy's adventure book to someone the camera
  never shows. He stops, smiles and says, "Your hands are warm tonight."
  Act 1 ends.
- **Arcs advanced:** Hollis (ends), Dace (his turn begins), the lead (the
  mentor is gone), everyone (the goodbye); from the sheets, Hedda (gets
  on the ship because she decided to, not because she was lent), Gil (he
  stays because he wants to, and it costs him a rule), Joss (watches his
  captain order the archers and try to stop them; says no to him in his
  own words), Aske (going the wrong way from her mother; the day she says
  "Veyne"), Oriel (force, not fire, on a tarred quay), Maud (the names in
  the brown bag go east with her), Tamsin (the mare on a ship; the book
  stays shut).
- **Leave for later:** where Wren is (the later twist); the prince (the
  king's companion is never named or shown; "warm hands" and no more);
  what Dace does with it (Act 2: he hunts the party and hunts for Wren);
  the Door Vow by name; Act 2's landing.

## Where each piece of the turn goes

- **Half HP (Dace):** he sends the archers to the long-house roof with
  one order: anyone on the plank. It is the order that kills his father,
  given an hour before, when he hadn't thought who would be last up it.
  (If the player never brings him to half HP the volley still comes: the
  order was given; the scene only shows him trying to call it back.)
- **The gangway (`ch08_victory`):** the hold is won, the ship is loaded,
  the party goes up the plank one at a time, Hollis last and backing up
  it. Dace comes down the salt street already shouting at the roof; the
  volley is in the air. Hollis falls to the quay at the plank's foot.
  Dace's hand is up; his Hounds hold; he walks down alone. Hollis says it
  to him; he looks at the lead's shoulder (it's up), then at his son;
  that's all. Dace:
  a box of dots, then "nobody shoots, let it go; there are no orders".
  The lead is pulled up the plank. The water opens; Dace on one knee with
  his father.
- **Without Hollis (the `@else`):** the lead is last on the plank. Dace
  holds the roof (he has standing orders about the exile: alive) and
  comes down alone with his reason first (the paper, the cart, Varenhall),
  then asks where his father is and reads the answer off the plank
  himself ("He's not, is he."; Dace was at Veyne Hall, so he may already
  know, and the lead says nothing). Dace lets the ship go first, on that;
  then the lead tells him about Wren from the plank, in plain lines, and
  Rue, if there, says what she saw, so it is a gift and not a trade. Dace
  stands with his hand still up and gives no order.
- **The deck (`ch08_sail`):** `@dies retainer`, and the grief of the
  people who watched (Tamsin's book, Maud's salve, Rue's bread), only
  when he died an hour ago; when he died chapters back, Hedda says
  she has never been east of the fjord instead, and the rest of the deck
  (the reply, the coat, the rule, "Veyne") is the same.
- **The dock (`ch08_dock`):** Dace alone, talking to the lead who isn't
  there: she walked off a field, and the Master of Vows told him she'd
  come back through a door; she never went through anything; she's
  walking about somewhere. An order about every keeper in gray between
  the coast and the capital. His turn begins; nothing of it is a plan. (He
  says nothing of his father here: after the deck's `@dies` the scene
  can't know whether the body is on this quay or three chapters back.)
- **The king (`ch08_closing`):** the book, the lamp, the warm hands.

### 7. The deck (`ch08_sail`)

- **Where:** the deck that evening, the coast a line and then not even
  that. The lead at the rail. Music: no change (`scene_departure` carries
  on from the quay; 0854).
- **Who:** the lead, Hollis's `@if` (holding `@dies retainer`), Tamsin,
  Maud, Rue, Hedda, Joss, Gil, Aske (each in an `@if`).
- **What happens (10–12):** narration: the deck, the coast going, the
  lead at the rail. **If Hollis died an hour ago:** Tamsin hasn't opened
  the book and isn't going to; he cheated at dice, did the lead know, one
  trick, forty years, the same one, and she let him, and he thought she
  didn't know. Maud had the salve in her hand; she had it out; she was
  three steps from him (one plain fact, no turn). Rue puts bread in the
  lead's hand without a word and sits down with her back to the rail
  (narration); did he eat, today, before, did anybody see him eat;
  anybody (a question, nothing else; three shapes for three people).
  **If he died chapters back:** Hedda has never
  been east of the fjord; Ragna has, once; she says there's no herring
  there (else narration: the coast is gone and nobody says anything).
  **Choice 2** (both paths): the lead's reply (act: I'll take the watch /
  blunt: go below, all of you / silent: ...); Hedda answers the first
  (no-- all right; wake her at the bell, outlaw; her cloak goes around
  the lead), Maud the second (yes, of course, she'll be below), Rue the
  third (fine, she'll sit here, nobody has to talk). Joss: give him the
  coat, the hole, he can do it now while there's light, he'd like to,
  please. Gil: rule fourteen is struck, he struck it on the quay; he was
  coming whatever the season said; he'd like that known. Aske, last:
  "Veyne. You are wet. Sit down." (her first "Veyne"; nobody remarks on
  it). The lead sits (narration). (The first draft's deck shaped three
  boxes the same way, "he did a small private thing", and had Joss's "he
  never once said the captain's name"; the cold read found the receiving
  line, so Rue asks a question and Joss asks for the coat.)
- **Purpose:** the goodbye, as grief for one man and a coast going; the
  dead turning up in other people's habits (the hole, "Veyne", the order
  to sit); the one hurting most saying least; everyone who could have
  stayed is on the boat because they chose it.
- **Changes:** *relationship* (Aske and the lead; Joss and the coat; Gil
  and his code), *stakes* (at sea, east).

### 8. The dock (`ch08_dock`)

- **Where:** the quay after dark, the ship's lantern out past the point
  and then gone; the Hounds at the street mouths, nobody having given them
  an order. Dace. Music: `scene_sad` (Claude's pick). Works whether or not
  Hollis died here: after `@dies` nobody is "there", so the scene can't
  show a body and doesn't say one way or the other.
- **Who:** Dace.
- **What happens (3):** Dace, to the lead who isn't there: she walked off
  a field; and the Master of Vows told him she'd come back through a
  door; she never went through anything; she's walking about somewhere. To the Hounds: which of them was at the
  Ashfields; none; then find him somebody who was; every keeper in gray
  between this coast and the capital; he wants their names by morning.
- **Purpose:** the outline's "Dace stands on the dock alone"; his turn
  begins as doubt and an order, not a plan.
- **Changes:** *information for the player* (Dace has stopped believing
  the door).

### 9. Varenhall (`ch08_closing`)

- **Where:** the king's private rooms at Varenhall, lamplight, a fire
  banked low, a book open on his knee. Played by 1065 after the victory
  scenes. Music: `scene_lamplight` (0071; 0854 adds the line).
- **Who:** the king. Nobody else is shown. Nobody else speaks.
- **What happens (5):** narration: the room, the lamp, the book, the king
  reading. The king reads a passage of a boy's adventure book aloud (a
  fox with a hurt paw that a boy has to carry out of a wood; ordinary,
  not pointed: a first draft's ship and storm echoed the chapter and a
  reader took the land for a symbol; a second's squire on a hundred steps
  and old knight asleep in his armor echoed Dace's steps and Hollis). He
  stops: that was always the part they wanted
  twice; shall he go on; no; that's enough for tonight; they have been
  very patient with him. He closes the book on his finger and reaches out
  of the lamplight. "Your hands are warm tonight."
- **Purpose:** the outline's closing clue, gentle, with the day's dead
  behind it and nothing explained.
- **Changes:** *information for the player* (the king reads to someone;
  warm hands, tonight).

## Continuity notes

- Saltmere: the quay, the fish steps, the salt street, the warehouse lane
  and the long warehouse roof are this sheet's words for 0854's map;
  nobody says a street's name as a proper noun. The League's dock men were
  paid to go home; no halberds appear. The tide and "an hour" are the
  scene's words for 0854's turn count; nobody says a number of turns.
- Ragna: in the roads off Saltmere since Chapter 5, "till the ice" (said
  by Hedda); had a man on the Ossary road; counts days (nine from Ossary);
  calls the lead "the exile"; sails with the ship as far as the Brennish
  coast; wanted Hedda home and bought her a cloak for the East's cold
  instead. She is on the ship during the battle (a speaker, never a unit)
  and speaks once from the deck.
- Hedda: "outlaw"; chose east in four words; "You asked." is her one
  trailing thought, about home. Her cloak goes round the lead on the deck.
- Hollis: the sword on his knees and the stone, the night before (his
  habit before every fight the lead has been in); sick on a cart once
  with Lord Veyne laughing; "my girl's on this side of the water, and the
  boy's wherever the boy is"; "Shoulder up tomorrow, kid"; takes Maud's
  salve. His "..." is rationed to where it costs him (his card: flat
  periods are his gruffness; the first draft had twenty-two). On the
  quay: "Guard up. You're dropping it." (Chapter 4's words, worn smooth)
  and "Your left." Dying: "boy"; your sister's alive; Rue saw her,
  walking off the field, after, with two keepers in gray; go and get her.
  He
  looks at the lead's guard and says nothing to them. His battle death
  quote (Classic) hands the lead the job: "you tell him what the girl saw
  on the plain."
- Dace: gives the roof its order at half HP ("anyone on the plank") and
  calls it back on the quay too late; walks down alone; a box of dots;
  "nobody shoots, let it go"; "there are no orders".
  Alone: "she never went through anything; she's walking about
  somewhere"; an order about keepers in gray. He closes a box by repeating
  its order three times in the chapter, not seven (a reader's count).
  He has not said "I'm sorry" (Act 3). He does not look at his father
  until his father is down.
- The lead: "You first." on the plank; without Hollis, three plain lines
  under forty characters: your sister's alive; she walked out of the
  fire; go and get her (and "two keepers in gray took her" when Rue is
  gone). Dace reads his father's death off the plank himself; the lead
  never says it (a first draft's "He's dead." read as cruel). The look,
  not a line, when Hollis dies.
- Aske: reads the bill and will not be helped; "east is the wrong way,
  for me"; letters for her mor in her pack, in her own hand; "Veyne" on
  the deck, for the first time (Chapter 1: "noble"; nothing since).
- Joss: the captain's hand on the bill; "I'm staying here"; asks for the
  lead's coat on the deck to do the hole, now, while there's light (the
  night before, Hollis said to let him, but the deck can't count on that
  scene having played, so Joss doesn't say so). The coat has had the hole
  since Chapter 2 (Dace noticed it in Chapter 4). His death quote: "Tell
  the captain I stood where I was put."
- Tamsin: the mare goes in the hold ("She's the width of two men. Plan
  for it."); the book stays shut; "he cheated at dice"; "Fourteen? It was
  eleven at the border."
- Maud: salve for the knee the night before, asked for with her
  qualifiers ("I think, possibly, you should let me, this once") and
  taken; "I had it in my hand. The salve. I had it out, I was three steps
  from him." on the deck; her "!" when Hollis falls. The brown bag of
  names went at the cold read (the scene was at its cap).
- Rue: "Is there food on a ship?"; "You lifted me in by the belt. I was
  fifteen."; bread in the lead's hand without a word; "Did he eat?
  ...Anybody?"; what she saw, said to Dace only if Hollis is gone
  (without "she said I was warm", which the first draft had and a reader
  took for the closing scene's hinge).
- Gil: rule fourteen (the circuit; the season) at the crossroads, struck
  on the deck ("I was coming whatever the season said. I'd like that
  known."). His
  death quote has no rule in it (stand up straight when they announce
  you; well
  struck). Rules so far in the scripts: one, two, four, six, nine

- Oriel: "May I come?"; force, not fire, on a tarred quay, and glad of
  the reason; the roll in his coat, to be left there with him ("I never
  put it down").
- Ragna: "exile"; "I count"; "I would like my hour"; "I asked."; "The

- The king: "I" in private; long sentences; present tense; gentle; never
  a name; never "dead". The book is a boy's adventure book (a fox with a

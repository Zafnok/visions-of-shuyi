# Continuity ledger

What each character knows, has done, and feels about the others, **as of a
point in the story**. Every script ticket updates this after its critique
pass (`story-writing` skill). When a scene needs to know "does X know Y yet?",
the answer is here.

Sections are added in order: *Start of Chapter 1* (below, written by 0701),
then *After Chapter 1* (0707), and so on. Don't edit an older section except to
fix an error; add a new one.

## Start of Chapter 1

### World state

- It's autumn, three years after the Ash Peace. The Unpaid roam the
  Thornmarch. The king hasn't appeared in public for a year.
- The Door plan is under way. Aurel came back Unfinished three years ago, and
  every death paid under the Door Vow, through Wren as bearer, makes him more
  real. Provoking a war with Brennmark is planned but hasn't started (Chapter
  2 starts it).
- Maud's autumn report reached Ossary. Crane recognised Rue and ordered the
  witnesses of the night at the mill silenced. Dace received the order, hired
  the Red Company under his Veyne seal, and sent the lead a warning letter by
  peddler.
- The Red Company took the old toll-fort by Harrowby's ford last night.

### What happened

- At dawn in the yard, Hollis mended the lead's boot by the fire while
  the lead split wood. He had not slept; the letter was in his coat, and
  he said nothing about it. He said he has never seen the sea in forty
  years on his feet, because it is the one thing the boy wrote home about
  from the war that he believed; and asked what the lead will do when
  the exile lifts (the lead's reply isn't recorded). The chapel bell rang
  an hour early. He sent the lead under the floor for the two real
  swords. **The lead bears steel from then on.**
- A paper was nailed to the chapel door in the night: the village burns
  at noon unless three people walk up to the toll-fort, "the exile, the
  old man, the girl with the burned hands". Maud found it and rang the
  bell. She heard a keeper's phrasing in the description of Rue and said
  only that it was how a keeper writes, and that her hands wouldn't hold
  it. Nobody asked.
- Tamsin took the paper: Harl's writing on somebody else's paper (he
  writes on the backs of things); he will do it, he burned a farm last
  winter for less. Rue offered to walk up herself, hands out of her
  sleeves; Tamsin asked what she had to do with any of it; "Why don't you
  ask whoever wrote it?"
- **Hollis took the letter out of his coat in front of everyone.** It
  came by peddler in the night, unsigned; in his words it said get out of
  the march by tonight, they are coming for everyone who was at the mill
  that night, burn this. Tamsin asked whose hand. Hollis didn't say; the
  lead did, to all of them: Dace's. "Aye. Burn it. Somebody. I can't."
  **Rue took it from his hand and burned it with her fire**, looking at
  the lead, not the letter. No letter exists.
- Aske had lain under the fort wall all night: they shot three of her
  deer from the wall, ate one raw and left two; men that hungry would not
  wait for noon. **No count of the fort's men was said.**
- Hollis: the ford is the only road out and they are sitting on it; the
  whole hamlet into the chapel; they have steel. Tamsin: Harl stands on
  walls to be seen; she would go around by the river and come at him from
  behind. The lead's reply (the plan) isn't recorded.
- The six fought the Red Company at the toll-fort. Harl is dead, and the
  rest of the company ran out of the gate when he fell (0845). Nothing
  in the hamlet burned.
- Harl's last words: first a plea not to hang the boys, they were
  hungry; then, to the lead: a lord bought you, your own wax on the
  paper, your own crest, silver up front; ask the new one in your hall
  what you cost him. If the lead fought him, he had already said
  "walk up, you were told, and we'd have done it quiet" and named the
  price, three hundred a head. To anyone else: forty men he brought home
  from a war, and the crown gave them a road. If Tamsin fought him: he
  had her down as dead in a ditch; he put her on her first horse and
  could put her off it; "And I held the horses at that farm after, and
  watched. ...Get off the wall."
- Tamsin went to the body after the seal, so as not to look at Hollis.
  She cut the red cloth off Harl's head, kept it, didn't look at his
  face, wrote one word in her ledger and shut it (the word isn't shown),
  and said he had picked her out of a muster line once, fourteen of them;
  then asked Maud how to spell "fourteen".
- Rue, going through the dead men's packs for food, found the order in
  Harl's coat (three lines, a price, and the seal in blue wax). The lead
  named the seal unprompted ("That's the Veyne seal"): the crest the lead
  cut off their coat. The last time she saw that wax it was on a cart at
  night full of children. "Your house's wax. Did you know?" The lead
  answered (a reply; not recorded); Rue wasn't convinced, whichever it
  was. **Rue has the order.**
- Hollis looked at the wax over the lead's shoulder, said nothing, and
  went and sat on the gate step with his back to everyone. Tamsin, to
  the lead: don't look at him, give him a minute; it's the boy's; the
  hall, the title and the wax all went to him with the king's thanks.
- Maud began to say she would write to Greywater Abbey and tell them Rue
  was no danger, and stopped: she can't write to the Abbey, not ever
  again. And past the boundary stone they hang the lead for that coat.
  She saw to the lead's hands.
- **Rue decided the road**, paper in hand: the seal has a hall, everybody
  knows where Veyne Hall is, she is going there, alone if she has to. The
  lead: "Not alone." Rue: in front of her, then, where she can see them.
  Aske had made everyone sit and eat before any of that (nobody had
  eaten since the bell; she did the deer). She said nothing about coming;
  she came.
- **Hollis said nothing about the hall or the boy**, at any point after
  the chapel. He got up off the step, cleaned the lead's sword on his
  own sleeve and gave it back. **Nobody buried Harl.** Down in the hamlet
  somebody found the bell rope.
- At dusk all six left Harrowby for Veyne Hall, to ask Dace.
- That night at Veyne Hall, Dace had two letters sealed on the desk,
  written before any news came; the news was Harl dead in the toll-fort
  and the exile walking north with five others. A Vigil messenger asked,
  for the Master of Vows, whether it was done, in writing; Dace handed
  him one of the two letters: "It's done. That says so." Alone, he
  locked the other, still sealed, in the desk drawer on top of the seal,
  sat in the old lord's chair, and said to the lead who wasn't there,
  by name, that they never once did as they were told. "Good."

### If a companion died in Chapter 1 (Classic)

Written by 0716, updated by 0737. Everything above describes a battle all
six lived through. In Classic a companion who fell in it is dead, and
`ch01_victory` plays without them. A companion who **retreated** (Casual)
is alive and was there: nothing below applies to them.

**True on every path**, whoever died: the lead saw Harl's order and the
Veyne seal on it; the lead knows that only the holder of the hall, Dace,
seals with it; the lead left Harrowby at dusk for Veyne Hall, an outlaw
past the boundary stone. Everyone alive left with the lead. Everyone
heard Dace's name by the chapel, before the battle.

**The dead.** If Hollis lived, he buried them (he says so over each). If
Hollis died, the lead buried them. Nobody buried Harl on any path. The
graves are at Harrowby's chapel.

**What Dace knows:** his report says Harl is dead and the exile walked
north with five others (`ch01_tbc`). It doesn't say who else died.
Whether and when Dace learns that his order killed someone (his own
father, if Hollis fell) is for later chapters.

What each death changes. Several can hold at once.

#### Hollis died

- His last words were to the lead: stay in your line; "The sea. I'd have
  liked to see it. Tell the boy that, if you see him. Not the rest." The
  one errand he ever gave the lead for his son.
- He never saw his son's seal on the order. Tamsin (if alive) said so:
  "The Captain never had to see this, at least."
- There is no letter: Rue burned it by the chapel. The lead dug the
  graves alone.
- Nobody asked the lead to go. Rue (if alive) set the road; otherwise the
  lead chose Veyne Hall alone.
- **His secret died with him:** the purse, the list and the stamped pass
  he burned. Nobody in the party suspects Dace of anything before the
  order. Only Rue (if alive) and Dace know what Dace did for Vosse.
- He never learns that Wren is alive. His Chapter 4 to Chapter 8 beats
  can't play (his sheet, "Classic permadeath"): the lead learns about Dace
  from the Greywater register, Rue and Dace himself; Dace hears about Wren
  from someone else, or nobody.
- The second sword from under the floor: not said where it is.

#### Tamsin died

- Her last words were about the mare: don't let her run back to the
  fort, "she'll go to him, she always--"; get my mare. She asked for
  nothing for herself. **Her ledger** was on her belt; nobody is shown
  taking it (who has it is a later chapter's call).
- Nobody went to Harl's body; nobody took the red cloth; the muster line
  was never told; nobody told the lead to give Hollis a minute.
- Nobody said whose seal it is aloud; the player is told whose hall and
  seal it is. The party had heard Dace's name by the chapel all the same.
- **Coldwell is known to nobody alive**: she and Harl were the only ones.
  The green heard only that Harl burned a farm last winter. If she
  fought Harl, "I held the horses at that farm" was said and nobody
  followed it up.
- Her horse stood saddled at the gate (Hollis, if alive: "Somebody see to
  it"). Who took it isn't said.
- Hollis (if alive) remembered her by the two crowns she owed him at dice
  and brought up every week.
- The party has nobody from the Unpaid. Her side quest and the Act 3
  rally need another way in.

#### Maud died

- Her last words: somebody ring the bell when it's over, or the people
  in the chapel won't come out; "Promise me the bell." (Somebody did: it
  rings at the end of the fort scene.)
- **The report is known to nobody alive in the party.** Rue never learns
  who told the Vigil where she was, unless a record says so (the Greywater
  register is Chapter 3's to decide). Maud's Chapter 3 confession can't
  happen.
- Nobody started a letter to Greywater Abbey; nobody said the Vigil
  knows; nobody said what the boundary stone means for the lead. The
  party left because of the order and the hall.
- Nobody saw to the lead's hands.
- Nobody said rites over any of the dead: she was the only one who knew
  them. The party has no keeper and no healer.
- Hollis (if alive) remembered that she couldn't lie and couldn't play
  dice and sat down to lose every week anyway, and dug her grave "deep",

#### Rue died

- **The cart was never mentioned.** The order was found by the lead;
  nobody in the party knows the Veyne seal was on a cart of children,

- Her last words: who is going to find "the others, from the barracks,

  barracks" and knows nothing of what it means.
- Tamsin's question by the chapel ("What do you have to do with any of

- **Nobody in the party knows Wren walked out of the Ashfields alive.**

- What Dace did at the mill must reach the lead another way (the

## After Chapter 2

### What happened

- The party walked from Harrowby all night and halted an hour above
  Kell's Ford before dawn, with no fire, because Maud sat down (her
  feet). Hollis kept her boot on; Tamsin said the mare would carry her
  and looked at the river bottom in the dark (forty acres of it, she was
  promised: said in front of everyone). Hollis's plan was the crown's
  bridge at the change of the watch, with the lead's coat inside out or
  off (the crest is gone, the hole isn't). **Aske knew when the watch
  changes and what the men do first**, and said it too exactly; Tamsin
  asked how she knew; "I hunt." Nobody followed it up. Rue gave the lead
  half a loaf and said she had counted (the lead had eaten nothing since
  the fort). Maud asked to be left behind; the lead answered (a reply,
  not recorded) and she got up. Then the sky over the ford, and a bell.
- At first light, the south end of Kell's Ford: thatch burning on both
  banks, the garrison's tower across the water with its bell going, a
  dead raider in the road in Brennish colors with a hayfork in him.
  Tamsin called it Brennish and wanted the far bank. **Aske turned the
  dead man over and read his boots: new, nailed, "Brennish cannot afford
  boots like this", and a badge sewn on that week.** She didn't know who
  they were; she knew they weren't from home. Hollis: the houses first,
  the road second, nobody waits for the bridge; Maud to stay back. **Maud
  said No**, in front of everyone, and went where the burned people
  were; Hollis: then stay behind me. Rue said the raiders weren't
  fighting anybody, only lighting roofs and walking to the next. Tamsin
  said the tower was watching and could see them.
- The party fought the raiders through the village and killed
  Lieutenant Silas Hendry at the ford.
- **In the battle** (what the triggers say, if they fired): Hendry told
  "Needle" to light the big house by the water; Joss offered the barn,
  then couldn't; Hendry shouted his name once and told him to stand by
  him and they'd say he was in the smoke; **Joss walked into the ford
  to his belt, let the torch go out, and stood there. Nobody went in
  after him.** To anyone, Hendry said walk off, you're not on my list.
  To the lead: he knew the coat, "Dace's exile", the captain tells
  stories about the hall when he's drunk and the lead is in them;
  he didn't want to have to tell the captain he'd killed them. To Aske:
  hers was the voice that came over his ford when he was fourteen, in
  those colors; "they did it to us first". Aske: her brother wore that
  badge and died in it. Dying, Hendry said a year's pay is owed, his
  mother gets it, **"tell the captain, he knows the house, he's eaten at
  it"**, and asked whether the boy was still in the river and for
  someone to get him out. Maud, at a house, took a stranger's arm and
  made him look at her face.
- **After:** the garrison came down from its tower at last, and a crown
  herald read from the bridge, to the village and not to the lead: at
  Kell's Ford this day Brennmark broke the Ash Peace, and the crown will
  answer; **the exile Veyne, "seen riding with the raiders", is
  proclaimed outlaw in every county, and so is anyone who shelters,
  feeds or hides them.** He went back without looking at the coat. The
  soldiers never crossed. Tamsin shouted at the tower that they were in
  it and it watched. Hollis: don't answer him; he stood by the lead.
- **Joss came up out of the ford**, wet to the belt, the badge torn off
  his coat, and said the herald's paper was in the lieutenant's bag a
  week ago, already written and sealed, **all but one line** (the one
  that names the exile), he carried the bag. He knew the
  lead from the paper and called them Your Grace. Rue asked why he was
  still wet and who told him to stand in the river; nobody. Aske: at
  home they will believe it, some will be glad, and her mor will hear
  that "we did this". Tamsin: a price on the lead by the end of the

### What happened

- **The road south.** A night's walking out of Veyne Hall, the road
  bending south toward the salt country. **Hollis walked at the back**,
  for the first time anyone could remember; Tamsin asked why, loud, and
  got "Since my knee. Walk." **Joss said it first**: somebody who can
  stop it has to be told by somebody who was there, and he'd say it to
  anyone who'd write it down. **Aske named who would**: the woman who
  signed the peace, Ragna Holt, whose name her hold spits at, who sits
  in Saltmere writing letters; and she did not want to go to Saltmere
  ("they sell to anyone there"; it wasn't why). Tamsin: Saltmere, then,
  two days over the downs. Maud, with the register under her robe,
  asked whether the sea is cold. Rue put bread in the lead's hand
  without looking at them; Gil took the ditch side of the road. Hollis,
  from the back, to the lead: "Shoulder, Veyne. It's dropped since the
  hall." Nothing else between them until Saltmere.
- **The Salt Road** (guards the path). A Hound post at the march stone
  where Ardeval ends and the League's country begins, salt carts turned
  back on the verge. Joss knew how a post stands and knew nobody out
  here; Tamsin: past the stone they're the League's problem, and the
  Hounds know it. Aske stood very still looking south over the ridge and
  said nothing. Hollis: Veyne in the middle, nobody runs for the stone.

  walking: "That's it, then. That's the sea. Huh. ...It's a lot of

  spoke, not even Tamsin. Maud had thought "a lake".

  when is there an eleven; Gil: it's a very old rule, from the fairs.
  Nobody pushed. Hollis: walk, we're not sleeping in this wind.
- **The Burnt Mill** (guards the path). A roofless mill on the last
  water before the plain, and in the millrace a fire with nobody
  holding it. **Rue named it: an echo**, somebody's fire vow still going
  after they died; it drinks fire; "I've got fire. That's all I've got."
  Tamsin: so how do you kill a fire, Sparks; Rue: you don't stand in it,
  start there. Hollis: spears, hit it and step back, Veyne on the dry
  side. Hedda had seen them from a ridge, never this close. They put it
  out ("like a lamp"); the millrace steamed. Rue stood with her hands
  out to the warm place ("I'm cold and it's warm"); **Aske watched her
  and looked away**. Hollis: eat.
- **Brennish Outriders** (optional). The army's scouts on the hill road
  saw a blue coat, Hound black and a hill girl walking between them.
  **Hedda shouted her name and the Holt hall**; they didn't believe a
  Ravn walks with southerners and a Hound. **One of them said, in Aske's

party, Hedda with it and Oriel alive. True on every path: Crane's name
on the road (if Maud lives) or his face in the barracks, the hands held
out at the road post, the empty barracks with the blankets aired,
Crane's lines (the Candles at the war; death is a door; the East shut
it), the warden between him and the room, the fight, her death, the
Hounds leaving with a carriage, and the cut to Crane and Dace.

- **Hollis dead:** nobody said "in and out" on the road, "spears out" at
  the post, "I know" at the gate, or "hoods up" in the street; nobody put
  a hand on a blanket (if Rue is also gone, nobody walks the row);
  **nobody asked Crane about Wren**, and "not lost" is unsaid; narration
  says there is no back up; nobody checked the lead's shoulder in the
  yard; nobody told Rue "no" about going back, or said "she's not with
  them"; nobody said nobody is going anywhere tonight.
- **Tamsin dead:** nobody asked who buys a bone; nobody argued rule six
  (narration says nobody has an answer for Gil); nobody shouted "then we
  take it" at the gate; the bone-walls line without Maud is unsaid;
  nobody said "out, then" in the yard.
- **Aske dead:** nobody counted hoods; **nobody asked "how old"**, and
      "My brother was twenty-two" is unsaid.
- **Maud dead:** nobody named the cracked bell or Crane on the road, so
  **the party first hears his name in the barracks only if 0853's name
  plate gives it** (the script's Crane never says his own name); nobody
  answered Tamsin about the bones; nobody noticed the hands at the post
  (narration says what the post was told); nobody said the words over
  the warden (narration says nobody did); the street's narration has no
  keeper walking ahead;   **nobody is called thorough**; nobody says what the
  second year teaches, and Crane says it himself, shorter; nobody says "don't lean on the walls" (Tamsin does); nobody
  offers to say the words in the yard (Rue's "you do that" is unsaid);
  **the barn is Rue's and Hedda's and short**: Rue says the old man said
  the East shut it, Hedda says where ships go, Hollis says nobody's going
  anywhere tonight; no second reply.
- **Rue dead:** nobody goes still at Crane's name; **the way in is
  narration's** (the yard and the steam door); nobody says the wardens
  are looking for her (narration: the post was told about hands); nobody
  says "him?" over the warden; **the ossuary gate is nobody's** (Oriel
  may still say he knows it; Hollis says nobody goes down there);
  nobody walks the row (Hollis touches every blanket to the end); Aske's
  question is unsaid; **no hands come up in the barracks**: the warden
  steps between Crane and the room anyway and sends him up; the
  warden's exchange on the stair can't play; nobody has the keys or says
  she couldn't count, and Maud's "I'll say the words" is unsaid; nobody
  says "then we go back for them", so Hollis's "no" is unsaid; **nobody
  tells Maud it was the hands**, and the barn is Maud's reply and the
  East alone; nobody watches her eat.
- **Joss dead:** nobody saw the banner leave; the party doesn't know the
  Hounds were in the city (the cut tells the player).
- **Gil missed or dead:** no rule six, on the road or in the yard; his
  battle lines can't play.
- **Hedda never joined (fell in the hold):** nobody asked about the bells
  or bowing; nobody put the pilgrims against the wall; the first-turn
  line without Oriel is unsaid; **nobody says where ships go** (Maud's
  "somebody there knows how" stands alone; Chapter 8's ship still comes
  from Ragna).
- **Oriel fell in the crossing (Classic):** nobody says "I know this
  gate"; nobody says the roll and the ceiling in the barracks; Crane
  says where the Candles went to nobody in particular; the first-turn
  line is Hedda's; his exchange with the warden can't play; nobody says
  "nineteen" in the yard, so Rue's "then we go back for them", Hollis's
  "no" and "she's not with them" are unsaid.

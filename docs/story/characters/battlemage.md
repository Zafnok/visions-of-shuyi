# Oriel Mast

Id: `battlemage` · [Name registry](../names.md)

## Role and class

- **Role:** playable companion from Chapter 6. The crown battle-mage who
  gave the Candles the order at the Ashfields, and the man who knows the
  prince burned on the king's own order.
- **Class:** **Sorcerer**, tier 2 (outline; its class vow is force,
  `progression.md`). Level, stats and talent: 0852. He has not sworn a
  fire vow since the Ashfields; whether and when he does again is for
  the act tickets (the outline: "never wants to burn anything again, and
  has to").
- **Age:** 41.
- **Joins:** deserts to the party at the Ashfields (Chapter 6).

## At a glance

A crown battle-mage for twenty years, licensed to fire, frost and force.
In the war's last three years he commanded the Candles' fire line: he
drilled the children, learned their names, told them stories, and on the
Ashfields he gave them the order to burn. That morning he saw the
prince's banner ride into the field against orders and sent a rider to
the king's tent to ask him to hold the fire. The answer came back: burn.
He gave the order. Afterward he walked the ash with the search parties
for two days, and Aurel's body was never found.

He has been kept on, quietly, at a desk. Now the king's army has brought
new Candles to the Ashfields, and he has been ordered to command their
line again. He deserts the night before.

## Want, need, flaw, secret

- **Want:** never to give that order again. Under it: to be punished,
  by someone who has the right.
- **Need:** to accept that living usefully is harder than being punished,
  and to stop asking the Candles to carry his guilt for him.
- **Flaw:** penance as a performance. He offers himself up ("Hit me,
  then. Go on.") so that somebody else has to decide what he deserves. He
  drinks.
- **Secret:** the king's order, and the body that was never found
  (outline: a clue for the midpoint twist; he reads it as an ordinary war
  mystery). He tells it in Chapter 6.
- **Pressure:** a deserter in wartime, and the crown knows exactly what
  he knows. Rue, who stood on his fire line, is in the party.

## Arc

- **Start:** a tired, guilty man who wants someone to burn him.
- **End of Act 1:** he has told the truth about the Ashfields, and Rue has
  refused to give him what he wants. He has to go on living with it.
- **Acts 2–3:** he burns again, once, to keep someone alive, in words of
  his own (the act tickets decide when). The Candles go home (outline,
  the ending), and he is one of the people who walks them there.

## Voice card

Written by 0743 (the `story-writing` skill, *Voices*). The sample lines
are illustrations of a situation, never lines for a script.

- **Age and experience:** forty-one; a gifted boy in the crown's schools,
  a battle-mage since twenty-one, a teacher of children for three years, a clerk
  for three more. He knows vows, drill, field orders, how to explain
  something to a frightened twelve-year-old, and the name of every child
  on his line.
- **Temperature:** cool and gentle; very tired.
- **Openness:** says terrible things plainly and quietly, and hides the
  small ones (that he's cold, that he's hungry, that he likes someone).
- **When scared:** calm, the drill voice, counting the line; polite.
- **Care signature:** fire, the ordinary kind. He builds and tends the
  camp fire, and puts the cold ones nearest it. He won't light it with a
  vow; he uses a flint, slowly, and swears at it.
- **Fault the others put up with:** drinks; apologizes to Rue until she
  tells him to stop; sleeps late; morbid; explains things nobody asked
  about, like a teacher.
- **Line length and habit:** medium, unhurried, courteous; asks
  permission ("May I?"). Says children's names: the Candles were never
  numbers to him (where Crane won't say a child's name, Oriel can't stop).
- **Words from his life:** the line, the drill, the children's names,
  "my third line", the flint, the cold, the search.
- **Won't say:** "I had no choice", or "orders". He refuses himself the
  excuse.
- **Punctuation:** periods and `...`; few questions; no `!` but one, the
  day he burns again.

**Sample lines** (one per situation; never copied into a script)

- *Under fire:* "Out of the smoke. All of you, out of the smoke, it moves
  with the wind and the wind is turning. ...Thank you."
- *At camp:* "No, I'll-- it's all right, I like the flint. It takes
  longer, I know. It's nearly-- there. You see? It only wants patience."
- *Lying:* "I don't remember their names. It was a long time ago, and
  there were so many." (He reads the roll every night.)
- *Grieving:* "The smallest one on the third line couldn't say his r's.
  I was 'Owiel' for a whole year, and I let him. ...I'm sorry. You didn't
  ask."

## Planted for after

- **Plan for after:** to keep a dog. Battle-mages move every season and
  he has never been allowed one. He has thought about what kind.
- **In his hands:** a flint and steel, and on bad nights the folded muster
  roll of his fire line, which he reads.

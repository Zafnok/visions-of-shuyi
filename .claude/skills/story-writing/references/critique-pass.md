# Story writing: the critique pass

From the `story-writing` skill: mandatory before committing a script.


A separate step from writing, done as an editor who did not write the
scene. Two parts.

**1. The cold read.** Give a fresh subagent only the scene's boxes, as
the player sees them: no beat sheet, no cards. Ask it: describe each
person the way you'd describe a coworker; which of them would you be
upset to lose, and why; who seemed to feel something; which line would
you remember; where would you press the key to skip ahead? Then give it
the scene again with the speaker names removed and ask who says each box.
Job descriptions instead of people, nobody it would miss, or a line it
remembers for being clever: the scene is robots. If it can't tell the
speakers apart, the voices are one voice. If it would skip, the scene is
too long there.

**2. The editor's list.** Fix every hit; if more than a couple of boxes
in a scene fail, redraft the scene from its card instead of patching:

- [ ] **Robots:** everyone competent, calm and right; nobody visibly feels
      anything; every box on task (no check-in, fussing or tease); an
      adult voice in a young mouth; the kind character never says
      anything kind; every speaker the same temperature; flat delivery
      (count the line endings: nearly all periods, no `!` from someone
      scared or glad, no `...` from someone who can't finish a thought).
- [ ] **The writer talking:** more than one box ending on a punchy line;
      a simile or crafted image under threat; a slogan or a moral; grief
      resolved by a symbolic object; a scene ending on a symbol.
- [ ] **Syrup** (the opposite failure): hugs and speeches about being a
      family, the cast praising the silent lead, therapy words, the big
      feeling said more than once per relationship per act, a plain line
      answered with a matching speech.
- [ ] **Reads like a report:** a box that only moves information, a fact
      nobody cares about, a number the map already shows, someone
      confirming what someone else just said.
- [ ] **A line-by-line rewrite:** for a rewrite, list the old and the new
      scene box by box (who speaks, what each box carries). If they match
      in order, the new draft is an edit of the old one: start again from
      the card.
- [ ] **Drag:** boxes before the trouble or after the turn; anything said
      twice; over the box budget.
- [ ] **Stock lines and tells:** a line any fantasy game could have
      ("The first one's always the worst"), "as you know" or "and you know
      it", "not X, but Y", lists of three, a speech under threat, the same
      thing called by three names, a quip under threat.
- [ ] Exposition dumps (more than 4 boxes of explanation in a row).
- [ ] Contradictions with beats, bible, ledger.
- [ ] Scenes where nothing changes.
- [ ] Lines over length budget; unknown expressions/speakers.
- [ ] The chapter advances the main plot AND at least one personal arc.

Then update `docs/story/ledger.md` with what changed for each character.

# Story writing: examples

From the `story-writing` skill, *Craft*.


Made-up characters, not Chapter 1's. Deserters under a captain named Hask
hold the bridge-house at Cullen's Ford, with the miller's family inside.
Wenna (16, a charcoal-burner's daughter) joined the company three days
ago. Osric is its sergeant: ten years in the war, a bad knee, says little.
Brother Anselm is a young Vigil keeper, kind, nervous and bad at fires.
Names are written out here; in a script they are name tokens.

**Before: what Nick rejected.** As a report (0728's style), the morning
of the attack:

```
Wenna: Eleven men in the bridge-house. I watched from the reeds all night.
  Four of them have crossbows. They change the watch at midnight.
Osric: Eleven is what I counted from the hill, too. They were good
  soldiers once. Now they charge a toll in blood.
```

As robots (0736's first samples): the same morning, and a grief scene
over a dead deserter Osric served with:

```
Wenna: A carter came up that road at dawn a penny short. One bolt off the
  wall and he went down like a felled birch. Wait for dark and I'll take
  us through the ford under the alders.
Osric: By dark they'll have two men sitting on that ford, listening for a
  splash. I taught them to.
Wenna: So they're your men.
Osric: They were the crown's.

Osric: Peregrine Cobbett. Two Ts.
Anselm[surprised]: Peregrine?
Osric: His mother had hopes.
> Osric takes the tally back and ties it to his own belt, beside one just
  like it.
```

Everyone is competent and calm, every line a move in an argument, the
grief a prop to decode and a dry button.

**After: three scenes, written from cards on a blank page** (Fable 5.1,
the blind test's winner, with the judges' fixes and the punctuation
balanced after Nick's notes).

1\. The night before (a quiet scene, 12 boxes):

```
> Late, the camp above the ford. Osric sits with his bad leg out straight,
  warming a lump of tallow in his fist over a boot. Wenna comes to the
  fire in her blanket. Brother Anselm sleeps sitting up.
Wenna: Can't sleep. The river's so loud. Ours at home, you can't even hear
  it from the house. How many of these have you done? Nights before, I
  mean. Hundreds, probably... Don't answer that.
Osric: Sit. Feet to the fire. Those your only boots? Give them here.
Wenna: They're good boots. Soft, so you can feel the ground. On a charcoal
  pile you have to, or you go through into the fire. ...What will you do
  after? When it's done and we're paid? If we're paid.
> She pulls them off and hands them over. He looks at her feet, then goes
  to work on the first boot.
Osric: My sister's at Thornby. Her privy's had a sack for a door since her
  man died. Eight years. I'm going to put a door on it, a good one, with a
  latch. She'll say it sticks.
Wenna[happy]: A privy door! Ten years in the war and you want-- no. No,
  it's good, it's a good plan. I'm not laughing. She'll love it.
Wenna: I keep thinking I'll be sick. Tomorrow, in front of everyone.
  That's what I'm scared of, if you want to know... being sick in front of
  you.
* kind: Stay close to me tomorrow.
  Wenna: I will. I was going to stick to somebody anyway... You and him,
    then.
* dry: Then be sick on Hask's men.
  Wenna: Don't. Don't make me laugh, I'll do it now.
* act: Eat something.
  Wenna: I can't, it'll just come back up, I-- all right. All right, I'm
    eating.
  Osric: Empty's worse for it. Chew.
Osric: You're on my left in the morning, I turn slow on that side. Now lie
  down. You don't have to sleep, just shut your eyes.
> She lies down where she is, with the blanket over her head. Osric starts
  on the second boot.
```

2\. The bridge (before the battle, played straight, 8 boxes):

```
> Dawn. Mist on the ford, and the slits of the bridge-house showing
  through it. Osric pushes himself up off one knee with his spear and
  pulls Wenna's shield strap tight.
Wenna: We could wait for dark? It's shallow below the mill, I've been
  looking at it, waist deep at the most. I've waded worse than that with a
  sack of charcoal on my back. They'd never see us!
Osric: And leave the miller's people in there all day, with Hask knowing
  we've come? He'll put them in the windows.
Wenna: I know. I know, I just-- you can't see what's behind a slit. That's
  all. You can't see what they've got...
Osric: No. Show me your hands.
> She holds them out. They shake. He closes them around the spear shaft,
  one and then the other.
Osric: Cold. That's all that is... Left of me, like I said. Brother,
  behind her. If she goes down, don't be nice, pull her back by the belt.
Anselm: Understood, Sergeant. By the belt. I've never... yes. Understood.
```

3\. Osric's death line in the battle, and that night (1 and 10 boxes):

```
Osric: Is Wenna on her feet? Somebody look! Nobody's on her right now,
  somebody-- is she on her feet?

> Night, by the mill. Wenna sits with her boots off and the tallow pot
  open beside her. The fire is nearly out. Brother Anselm comes with bread
  in both hands.
Anselm: The miller's wife is baking. For everyone, she insisted. I brought
  you some... And I did the fire, I'm sorry about the fire, I can never
  get them to-- you don't have to eat it.
Anselm: I said the words over him. I lost my place once and had to begin
  again, but they were said. All of them, properly. I wanted you to know
  that.
Wenna: He took my boots off me last night. Didn't even ask. Just "give
  them here."
Anselm: He did that to me as well. The day I joined. I thought I was being
  punished for something... Wenna, if you'd like to talk about him, I--
* act: Give me the other boot.
  > Wenna hands over the boot and the tallow.
  Wenna: Warm it in your hands first. He-- you warm it first, or it just
    sits on top.
* blunt: Eat something. We march at first light.
  Wenna: ...Yes.
  Anselm: First light. Yes. Of course.
* silent: ...
  Wenna: You can both go. I'm all right. Go on. I'm all right.
  Anselm: I'll just sit a minute, then... I won't say anything.
Wenna: Who's going to do his sister's door?
Anselm: His-- sorry. Whose door?
Wenna: Nothing... Get some wood. Split it first, it's only wet on the
  outside.
```

What makes it work:

- **People with lives.** Osric has a widowed sister and a privy door to
  fix; Wenna's fear is a sixteen-year-old's (being sick in front of the
  man she looks up to). She talks too much and catches herself.
- **Care on the body** carries the warmth in every scene: the boots, the
  tallow, "Show me your hands", "Chew", "pull her back by the belt". None
  of it is a joke, so it stays in the straight scene before the battle.
- **Small feelings said, the big one never.** "That's what I'm scared
  of", "I'm not laughing". Nobody says what Osric and Wenna meant to each
  other.
- **They disagree like people who like each other.** She hedges and half
  agrees; he overrules with a reason, then takes her hands.
- **Somebody is bad at things.** Anselm can't light a fire and lost his
  place in the rite, and says so.
- **The death line is about the living.** Osric dies asking whether Wenna
  is on her feet.
- **Grief as a habit passed on, and a plan nobody will finish.** Wenna
  teaches his tallow trick in his words, gives orders the way he did, and
  asks about the door. Anselm didn't hear about the door, so only she and
  the player know. Nothing explains it.
- **The humor comes from people**, in the quiet scenes only: the privy
  door, "Don't make me laugh, I'll do it now".
- **Punctuation in proportion.** Three `!` in the whole arc, each at a
  peak (her laugh, her plea, his dying shout). Wenna's nerves show in
  questions, trailing thoughts and cut-offs; Anselm trails off; Osric
  ends flat and trails off once, holding her shaking hands.
